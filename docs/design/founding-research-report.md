# Build DonationValues from observed thrift shelf prices

**Decision:** DonationValues.org should be a clean-room, first-party price survey of what US thrift and consignment stores actually charge for used clothing and household goods, published as immutable tax-year editions under CC0 with the observations behind every value, and DeductIt should retire its current 1,757-item table rather than try to open-source it. The law points unambiguously at this design: Pub 561 says clothing valuation "does not lend itself to fixed formulas or methods" and that "the price that buyers of used items actually pay in used clothing stores, such as consignment or thrift shops, is an indication of the value," Pub 526 says percentage-of-replacement-cost formulas "aren't acceptable," and Form 8283 column (i) lists "Thrift shop value" as a sanctioned method label ([IRS Pub 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561); [IRS Pub 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526); [IRS i8283](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8283)). Fifteen years of Tax Court clothing cases were all lost on substantiation, never on a reasonable thrift value, so the dataset's job is to be the "other evidence" Pub 561 demands and to push users into the substantiation chain. No resale marketplace will license redistributable sold-price data (eBay's API license forbids deriving category averages and redistributing content), the Goodwill and Salvation Army guides and the ItsDeductible values are copyrightable editorial estimates under *CDN v. Kapes*, and the DeductIt table is unlicensed whether it was exported from ItsDeductible or hand-compiled; the only lawful, redistributable source is observations the project collects itself under a Data Contributor Certificate ([eBay API License Agreement](https://www.edp.ebay.com/join/api-license-agreement); [CDN v. Kapes](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.1256.html)). The methodology is a BLS-style checklist survey (outlets → item specs → five tags per visit), a median headline with a p25–p75 band, three published tiers mapped to brand tier rather than invented condition multipliers, minimum-sample rules with parent inheritance, and AI agents confined to classification, normalization and flagging, never pricing. Licenses: Apache-2.0 for code, CC BY 4.0 for methodology, CC0 for data, plus a registered "DonationValues" trademark. The site is static, evidence-first and methodology-led, with a published governance document, an advisory council of practitioners, and adoption aimed first at vendors with no valuation tool and at CPAs/EAs who cite workpapers. The roadmap runs from attorney review in late 2026 to a partial first edition `DV-2027` in mid-2027 and a full-window `DV-2028` on the regular December cadence.

## IRS rules and fifteen years of case law reward observed thrift prices, not formulas

The regulatory definition is the willing-buyer/willing-seller test in Treas. Reg. §1.170A-1(c)(2); the oft-quoted "market in which such item is most commonly sold to the public" sentence is not there but in the estate-tax regulation §20.2031-1(b), and the methodology page should cite each correctly because a reviewer will check ([26 CFR 1.170A-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.170A-1); [26 CFR 20.2031-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2031-1)). Pub 561 (revised December 2025) applies the test to clothing by example: the FMV of a used garment given to the Salvation Army "would be the price that typical buyers actually pay for clothing of this age, condition, style, and use," and it ranks evidence as the item's own arm's-length sale price, then comparable sales weighted by similarity, timing, arm's-length status, **same market area** and normal market conditions, then price guides only as "clues" that "should be supported by other evidence," and replacement cost almost never ([IRS Pub 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561)). Pub 526 is blunter for household goods: "formulas (such as using a percentage of the cost to buy a new replacement item) aren't acceptable," which rules out the "30% of original price" fallback that Goodwill Northern New England publishes ([IRS Pub 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526); [Goodwill NNE](https://goodwillnne.org/donate/how-to-donate/donation-value-guide/)). The Form 8283 instructions add the only IRS sentence sanctioning online references, that donors "can also review classified ads in the newspaper or on the Internet to see what similar products sell for," which supports sold prices, not asking prices ([IRS i8283](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8283)).

The statutory gate is §170(f)(16): no deduction for clothing or a household item unless it is "in good used condition or better," with a single-item exception above $500 that requires a qualified appraisal and Form 8283 Section B; "household item" includes furniture, furnishings, electronics, appliances and linens but excludes food, art and antiques, jewelry and gems, and collections ([26 USC 170](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/170)). Nothing in the Code, the regulations, Pub 526, Pub 561 or §1.170A-16 defines "good used condition," so the dataset must define it operationally. The substantiation tiers are cumulative and strict: a receipt or reliable written records (including condition and "how you figured the FMV") under $250, a contemporaneous written acknowledgment at $250 and above, Form 8283 Section A with acquisition date, manner and cost basis above $500, and a qualified appraisal plus Section B above $5,000, where "similar items" (clothing, furniture, books, toys, kitchenware and so on) are aggregated across every donee for the year ([26 CFR 1.170A-16](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.170A-16); [26 CFR 1.170A-13](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.170A-13)). Penalties for overvaluation are 20% when the claimed value is 150% or more of the correct figure and 40% at 200%, but only when the resulting underpayment exceeds $5,000, and §6664(c)(3) strips the reasonable-cause defense for charitable property unless the value rested on a qualified appraisal plus a good-faith investigation; a value guide is therefore evidence for a good-faith investigation, never a safe harbor ([26 USC 6662](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6662); [26 USC 6664](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6664)).

The case law is the strongest design input because it is so one-sided. **No federal opinion has rejected a per-item value because it came from a Goodwill, Salvation Army or ItsDeductible guide; every clothing and household case was lost on substantiation or on an obviously wrong market.** In *Ohde* the main evidence was an ItsDeductible spreadsheet with "every item rated 'high'," no cost basis and no contemporaneous preparation; the court cut $145,250 to $250 and sustained the penalty ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2017/07/25/tax-court-denies-deduction-for-donation-of-20000-household-items/26389/)). In *Grainger* original Talbots price tags were rejected because "no rational buyer having knowledge of the relevant facts" would pay more than the discounted price, all clothing was aggregated as similar items above $5,000, and Goodwill receipts that said only "clothing" were not valid acknowledgments ([The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2018/oct/inflated-charitable-contribution-deduction-denied.html)). In *Bass* 173 sub-$250 trips did not avoid aggregation; clothing of $13,852 plus $11,594 across two charities required a qualified appraisal and the whole deduction fell ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2023/05/16/sizing-up-a-charitable-deduction-for-clothing-when-is-an-appraisal-required/79750/)). In *Besaw* (2025) the court believed the donations happened and still disallowed everything because none of the receipts described the items ([T.C. Summ. Op. 2025-7](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/TC_Summ_Op_2025-7.pdf)). The one court that set its own number, *Stamoulis*, said the thrift store's actual resale prices "would certainly be relevant and persuasive evidence," and the Oregon Tax Court in *Copeland* (2025) rejected Amazon-new prices and Facebook Marketplace listings that were not matched item-for-item ([Stamoulis](https://law.onecle.com/tax/2007/stamoulis.sum.wpd10.html); [Copeland](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/copeland-v-dep-t-1091465844)). The IRS Chief Counsel's own case digest lists *Smith* and *Kunkel* as the clothing precedents examiners rely on, both decided on missing acknowledgments, appraisals and condition evidence ([CCA 202020002](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/202020002.pdf)). The Taxpayer Advocate's litigation review for 2017–2018 found the IRS prevailed in 24 of 29 charitable cases and in all 10 pro se cases, with substantiation at issue in 18 and valuation in only 4 ([TAS 2018 MLI #8](https://www.taxpayeradvocate.irs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/ARC18_Volume1_MLI_08_CharitableDeductions.pdf)).

Three design consequences follow. First, defensibility is about traceability: a value with its store, date, region, condition and sample size behind it answers the *Stamoulis* and *Copeland* critiques and satisfies the regulation's requirement to record "the method utilized in determining the fair market value" ([26 CFR 1.170A-13](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.170A-13)). Second, the dataset must force heterogeneity and the substantiation chain downstream: uniformly "high" ratings read as a credibility problem, and the $5,000 similar-items cliff is where real donors lose everything. Third, the audience is narrowing. SOI data for tax year 2023 show about **1.84 million returns reporting clothing on Form 8283 ($5.8 billion, average $3,152 per return)** and 1.19 million reporting household items, while 6.5 million returns claimed a noncash deduction on Schedule A against only 2.9 million Forms 8283 in 2022, implying roughly 3.6 million filers at or below $500 who need a simple, defensible number and nothing more ([SOI Figure A TY2023](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-soi/23infigacc.xlsx); [IRS Pub 5337](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p5337--022025.pdf)). From tax year 2026 the OBBBA adds a 0.5%-of-AGI floor for itemizers and a cash-only non-itemizer deduction, so noncash gifts never qualify for the new §170(p) deduction and bunching will push more donors across the $5,000 appraisal threshold ([26 USC 170](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/170); [Taft Law](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/charitable-giving-after-the-obbba-the-2026-outlook/); [NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/you-make-the-call-sept-24-2026/)). The site's IRS primer must say all of this plainly.

## DeductIt's 1,757-item table is unlicensed either way and must be retired, not opened

The repository tells two stories about where its values came from. The ETL script `scripts/import_fmv_data.dart` names its input `~/Downloads/itsdeductible_fmv_guide.csv`, and `docs/fmv-database.md` says the data is "based on the ItsDeductible guide" with an update procedure that reads "Export new CSV from ItsDeductible"; yet the user-facing help text in `frontend/lib/help/help_content.dart` tells users the values are "based on Salvation Army and Goodwill guidelines," a claim nothing in the repo supports. The guides research found no public evidence that Intuit ever offered its valuation database for download: the 2022 and 2025 shutdown notices offered users exports of their *own* entries, the post-shutdown Intuit account download returns a user's personal data in a `SharedData` folder with no download-specific license, and the only legal text is Intuit's site-wide Terms of Use ([Intuit help article](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/tax-topics/help/manage-your-intuit-data/00/971878); [Deductible Duck](https://deductibleduck.com/what-happened-to-itsdeductible/)). The reconciliation is therefore simple: **the CSV was either reconstructed from ItsDeductible's interface or a user export, or hand-compiled from some mix of guides, and in every scenario it carries no license.** The internal evidence favors the ItsDeductible origin, because the asset's structure (a "High" value with "Medium" at a flat 70%, no "Low" tier, 22 categories, "Subcategory: Item" naming) matches ItsDeductible's High/Medium/Low scheme with Low suppressed after the 2006 Pension Protection Act, and because the app's own release notes call the bulk-entry grid "ItsDeductible-style." ItsDeductible's values were Intuit's "established values" produced by "blending data gathered from various thrift and consignment stores," earlier supplemented by eBay closed-auction feeds under a licensing program, which is exactly the editorial-estimate category that *CDN v. Kapes* and *CCC v. Maclean Hunter* hold copyrightable even though each number looks like a fact ([ItsDeductible help, now 404](https://itsdeductibleonline.intuit.com/help/addoreditothertrackerhuid.html); [tins.rklau.com](https://tins.rklau.com/2006/02/i-want-my-money-back/); [CDN v. Kapes](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.1256.html); [CCC v. Maclean Hunter](https://www.quimbee.com/cases/ccc-information-services-inc-v-maclean-hunter-market-reports-inc)). Intuit paid roughly $10 million for Income Dynamics in July 2003 and the 2002 product rested on thirteen years of thrift and consignment surveys, so the table has identifiable commercial value and an identifiable owner ([Intuit 10-K FY2003](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000089161803004854/f90545e10vk.htm); [Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/software-aids-taxpayers-who-donate-goods/)).

The consequences for DeductIt are immediate and separate from DonationValues. The asset was committed once on 2025-10-09 and never updated; it carries no license, attribution or source text; and `frontend/assets/terms.md` already hedges that FMV data "may be based on third-party sources." The help copy should be corrected now to something accurate and neutral ("DeductIt includes estimated values for common items; you are responsible for determining fair market value"), because a false provenance statement is worse than no statement. The table should not be published, uploaded to the open dataset, or used to seed, calibrate or benchmark DonationValues' values; it may continue to ship inside DeductIt only until the first DonationValues edition can replace it, and counsel should weigh whether even that interim use warrants an earlier switch to a thin, clearly independent placeholder. Nothing in the repo prevents a swap: the loader reads `{version,itemCount,categories,items:[{c,d,h,m}]}` from `fmv_data.json.gz`, ignores unknown keys, and stores description text, category, quality and the unit value on each donation record, so replacing the file changes only new entries and the quality-toggle re-resolution of old ones.

What a drop-in replacement must preserve, from the repo survey, is precisely this: a gzip JSON with integer cents in `h` and `m`; `d` as the only item identifier, with the text before the first colon treated as the Browse subcategory (659 colon-less items, 37.5%, are unreachable through Browse today and `Portable Audio & Video` renders empty); the 22 category names, which are stored on donations as `itemCategory` and drive both Browse and TurboTax batching; exactly two condition tiers (`ItemQuality.high`/`medium`) end to end in the enum, Firestore field, CSV export and ItsDeductible import mapping; and exact description equality for `findByDescription`, which the cart uses to swap tiers and to lock standard values. Four descriptions are duplicated across categories today (`Bowl`, `Saucer`, `Walker`, `DVD Player`), and the custom-item code derives the other tier at 0.8/1.25 while the bundled data uses 0.70, so a replacement with independently measured tiers needs no code change but will produce different ratios per item, which is a feature.

## No marketplace feed is redistributable, so values come from first-party observation under a clean room

The copyright line is clear and favorable to original collection. *Feist* holds that facts are unprotectable and that "a subsequent compiler remains free to use the facts contained in another's publication," while *CDN v. Kapes* holds that a guide's estimated prices are protected expression precisely because they are not "prices paid by dealers" but the publisher's "best estimate" produced by judgment rather than "a set formula or rule," and the court expressly said the line "allows CDN's competitors to create their own price guides" ([Feist](https://cyber.harvard.edu/property00/alternatives/feist.html); [CDN v. Kapes](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.1256.html)). An observed tag price, a sold price or a volunteer's field report is a fact; a Salvation Army "$3–$12" range or an ItsDeductible "$13 high / $7 medium" is an estimate. Copying the guides' numbers is the infringement risk; collecting prices and computing one's own statistics is the route the opinions leave open. The same logic says DonationValues' derived values may themselves be thinly protected if judgment enters, or unprotected if produced purely by formula, which is why an explicit CC0 dedication matters: it removes the uncertainty for reusers regardless of which way a court would rule ([CC wiki: Data](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Data)).

The marketplace route is closed for publication. eBay's API License Agreement requires "express prior written permission" to use eBay Content in any way that "enables derivation of" site-wide statistics including "Average selling price or gross merchandise sold for any eBay category," prohibits using eBay Content "to suggest or model prices," prohibits training algorithms or machine learning on it, requires intermediate copies to be deleted and content to be no more than 6–24 hours stale, and makes Restricted API data non-distributable "in raw or aggregated form"; the sold-history Marketplace Insights API is invite-only with a 90-day lookback ([eBay API License Agreement](https://www.edp.ebay.com/join/api-license-agreement); [eBay Marketplace Insights](https://developer.ebay.com/api-docs/buy/marketplace-insights/overview.html); [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2025/07/18/ebay-restricts-developers-from-using-its-data-to-train-ai/)). Poshmark's terms forbid any "scrape, harvest, crawl" and Mercari's prohibit "any robot, spambot, spider, crawler, scraper" ([Poshmark Terms](https://poshmark.com/terms); [Mercari Prohibited Conduct](https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/topics/account/policies/prohibited-conduct/)). CFAA exposure for logged-out scraping of public pages is low after *Van Buren* and *hiQ*, and *Meta v. Bright Data* and *X v. Bright Data* show logged-out scraping and even resale of public data can escape contract and state-law claims under particular terms, but *Southwest v. Kiwi* shows site terms can still support an injunction, and a public, attributable project with a sponsor LLC is a soft target for a cease-and-desist that worsens the contract analysis ([Van Buren](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/19-783); [hiQ v. LinkedIn](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/publications/client-alert-data-scraping-in-hiq-v-linkedin-the-ninth-circuit-reaffirms-narrow-interpretation-of-cfaa); [Meta v. Bright Data](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [X v. Bright Data](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2024/05/district-court-adopts/district_court_adopts_broad_view_of_copyright_preemption_in_data_scraping_case.pdf); [Southwest v. Kiwi](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2021/10/tos-supports-injunction-against-web-scraping-southwest-airlines-v-kiwi.htm)). The classification is therefore: no platform qualifies as an official redistributable source; eBay API data is at most internal calibration under a written exception that the license text makes hard to obtain; and raw scraped listings are never published. Online sold prices enter the design only as a clearly labelled secondary series collected by contributors who report individual sold listings they personally observed (a fact, like a tag), used within brand tier for validation and sparse-cell fallback, and never blended into the headline.

The lawful, redistributable sources are two. The first is crowdsourced and paid in-store observation: a posted price is a fact, the store's only recourse is a premises policy (ask the collector to stop or leave), and the precedent projects show the contributor model, with Open Food Facts requiring contributors to "possess the reproduction rights," to have taken photos themselves, and to accept that "the public character of contributions cannot be revoked," and Wikidata choosing CC0 to avoid "attribution stacking" across millions of editors ([Open Food Facts terms](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use); [Wikidata:Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing); [FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/?p=43937)). The second is written data agreements with thrift operators; Goodwill is roughly 150 independent regional nonprofits, so a regional operator can grant a price-board feed or in-store collection permission without a national negotiation, and the BLS precedent of buying "a large volume of price data" from one apparel vendor shows a statistical agency accepting exactly this kind of supplementary feed when documented ([MoneyTalksNews](https://www.moneytalksnews.com/the-best-american-cities-for-thrift-shopping-in/); [BLS HOM CPI: Data sources](https://www.bls.gov/opub/hom/cpi/data.htm)).

The clean room is a written policy, not a hope. The pipeline ingests only first-party observations and licensed feeds; nobody drafting the taxonomy or writing estimation code works from an ItsDeductible export, a DeductIt asset dump or a charity guide, and the category list is drafted from the IRS "similar items" categories and Google Product Taxonomy rather than from any guide's item list, because even a guide's selection of items is thinly protectable ([Google Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324436)); every observation records contributor, place, time and method; a dated policy prohibits importing third-party guide values; and any benchmarking against existing guides is a separate evaluation whose output is a report, not data fed back into the model. The DeductIt crosswalk between legacy description strings and `dv_id`, needed for the compatibility period, lives in DeductIt's private repository, not in the open dataset, and expires with the compatibility file.

## A checklist survey with medians and three published tiers is the methodology

The sampling template is the CPI, which prices roughly 80,000 goods and services in about 23,000 outlets through a two-stage outlet → unique-item probability sample, locks each priced item down with an entry-level-item checklist "necessary to identify the item" on later visits, substitutes the closest checklist match when an item disappears, imputes missing quotes by cell-relative, class-mean or carry-forward rules, and accepts alternative data from single vendors where documented ([BLS HOM CPI: Design](https://www.bls.gov/opub/hom/cpi/design.htm); [BLS HOM CPI: Calculation](https://www.bls.gov/opub/hom/cpi/calculation.htm); [BLS HOM CPI: Data sources](https://www.bls.gov/opub/hom/cpi/data.htm)). The index arithmetic does not transfer (a guide needs levels, not changes), but the checklist discipline, the outlet frame, the substitution and imputation rules and the stated cadence all do. The credibility template is the used-car guides Pub 561 already accepts as non-official references: KBB publishes regional values "updated weekly" across more than 100 regions and J.D. Power publishes distinct trade-in, retail and loan values from more than 12 million retail transactions with three condition grades, and BLS sources every CPI used-vehicle price from J.D. Power ([kbb.com](https://www.kbb.com/whats-my-car-worth/); [J.D. Power](https://www.jdpower.com/black-book-values); [BLS HOM CPI: Data sources](https://www.bls.gov/opub/hom/cpi/data.htm)). What to copy is the shape, not the opacity: a few value types keyed to transaction context, coarse condition tiers with plain definitions, an as-of date, regional modifiers as an index to a national base, and a short public statement of sources and volumes.

The market definition is **the full tag price on the US thrift and consignment shelf**, which is the market Pub 561 names and the market the donee itself sells into. Goodwill regions rotate colored tags with one color at 50% off (and sometimes 75%) each week and pull the discounted color afterward, so a share of transactions clear below tag; the design records markdown status as a field, publishes the tag-price statistic, and footnotes the markdown share rather than silently discounting, with a transaction-weighted alternative documented for the methodology comment period ([Goodwill of North Georgia](https://goodwillng.org/color-of-the-week/); [Goodwill NNE shop](https://goodwillnne.org/shop/)). Thrift pricing is category-flat with brand exceptions: two independent guides put a plain men's shirt in a $3–$12 band while Goodwill routes branded goods to a boutique tier at $3.99–$24.99, and the only large revealed-price study of garments (8,280 items across four EU countries) found "no statistically significant link between physical condition and resale value" once an item passes the rewearable threshold, with "brand recognition, fashion trends and perceived desirability" setting price and only 5–10% of garments being premium ([Salvation Army guide](https://satruck.org/donation-value-guide); [Goodwill NNE](https://goodwillnne.org/donate/how-to-donate/donation-value-guide/); [ABC15 Goodwill boutiques](https://www.abc15.com/the-now/goodwill-boutiques-offer-high-end-items-for-a-fraction-of-the-cost); [Fashion for Good Rewear](https://www.fashionforgood.com/case-study/sorting-for-circularity-rewear/)). For electronics, condition does move price: used iPod Classics on eBay averaged $116.70 against $220.90 new, and laptop condition categories differed significantly ([IJPE 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925527315000407); [JDMDC laptop study](https://jdmdc.com/index.php/JDMDC/article/download/35/27)). The design therefore carries a **brand-tier axis (basic / mid / premium) as the primary driver for clothing** and a working/complete flag for household goods and electronics, and it treats "good used condition or better" as the inclusion threshold for observations: collectors survey only saleable shelf stock, which the chains have already screened, so every observation is by construction an item the IRS would allow.

### Item schema

Three tables make up an edition. The taxonomy table defines item specs; the observations table holds facts with provenance; the values table holds the derived statistics. All three are CSV with a Frictionless Table Schema in `datapackage.json`, and the field lists below are the recommended v1.

| Table | Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `items` | `dv_id` | string, stable | e.g. `dv:clothing.mens.shirt.dress`; never reused or renamed; retirements keep the row with `status=retired` and `superseded_by` |
| | `label` | string | Donor-facing name |
| | `irs_group` | enum | IRS "similar items" group: clothing, furniture, household_items, electronics, appliances, books, toys, kitchenware, linens, sporting_goods, other; excluded groups (jewelry, art, collections) flagged `excluded_from_household` |
| | `category`, `subcategory`, `item_type` | string | Project taxonomy; `subcategory` is what the DeductIt Browse convention needs before the colon |
| | `gender_age` | enum | mens, womens, boys, girls, toddler, infant, maternity, unisex, n/a |
| | `parent_id` | string | For inheritance when a spec is under-sampled |
| | `google_product_taxonomy_id` | int | Primary crosswalk; `gpc_brick` optional secondary |

### Condition and tier handling

The published tiers are percentiles of the same observed distribution, defined in plain language that a collector can apply in the aisle and a donor can apply at home: **"Good, basic brand" = p25** (store brand, fast fashion or unbranded, ordinary wear), **"Good, typical" = p50** (the central estimate and the default a donor should use), and **"Excellent / premium brand" = p75** (designer, technical or boutique-routed, looks new). Nothing is published below p10, because items worse than good are non-deductible or need an appraisal above $500 and so need no value. This reproduces the High/Medium semantics donors learned from ItsDeductible and the low/high structure of the charity guides, but with a reproducible definition, a sample size and a band. Household goods and electronics get a separate working/non-working value only when both groups have n ≥ 10 and their medians differ by more than 25%; otherwise the working flag is reported but not split. For DeductIt's two-tier model, `h` maps to p75 and `m` to p50, and when the app adds a third tier it maps to p25. Showing a wide bare range invites donors to pick the top, which is what the risk-communication literature predicts ("imprecise ranges enable motivated reasoning"), so the per-item page leads with the p50 figure and labels the band as "the middle half of observed thrift prices" ([Dieckmann, Peters & Gregory 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/riskan/v35y2015i7p1281-1295.html)).

### Statistical defaults and minimum-sample rules

Thrift prices cluster on a few price points with a long right tail of boutique outliers, which is the textbook case for the median: NIST's handbook notes that "for data with extreme values in the tails, the median provides a better estimate of location than does the mean," with trimmed and winsorized means as alternates that trade robustness for efficiency ([NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda351.htm)). The defaults, all of which the methodology page must label as the project's own judgment calls because no thrift-specific literature supplies them, are: headline = median of tag prices per item spec per edition window; band = p25–p75; mode reported alongside because prices are discrete; 95% bootstrap percentile CI on the median with B = 2,000 and the RNG seed recorded in the edition metadata; outliers never deleted but flagged for review beyond Tukey fences (1.5 × IQR) or 3 × the spec median and kept in the raw file; rounding to the nearest $0.50 below $20 and nearest $1 above, with cents retained in the raw table. Minimums: a **national value needs n ≥ 20 observations from ≥ 5 distinct stores in ≥ 3 metros**; a **regional (Census division) value needs n ≥ 10 from ≥ 3 stores**; below either threshold the spec inherits its parent's value with `inherited_from` set, the CPI "class-mean" analogue. Regional values are computed as an index to the national median and exposed in the UI only when |index − 1| > 0.15 and the regional minimum is met, mirroring KBB's regional modifiers and BEA's price-parity form; otherwise the national value is the default, which matches how Pub 561 and donors treat thrift stores as one market ([FRED NYMPRPPGOOD](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NYMPRPPGOOD)). The online secondary series is computed within brand tier, net of platform fees of roughly 13–20% and shipping, scaled by a per-category venue factor calibrated only on cells where both series exist, published with its CI, and withheld wherever the factor cannot be estimated; selection bias toward the top 5–10% of garments, not fees, is the dominant distortion, so brand-tier matching is what makes the series usable at all ([Closo cost guide](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/thrifting-for-resale); [Fashion for Good Rewear](https://www.fashionforgood.com/case-study/sorting-for-circularity-rewear/)).

The in-store protocol is quasi-systematic to defeat cherry-picking: for each assigned spec a collector records the first five tags encountered in a fixed walk order, records a substitute with the closest-match rule when the spec is absent, and photographs the tag (cropped, no people, no receipts). The outlet frame is stratified by Census division × chain with at least three metros per division, two chains per metro and one store per chain-metro, a third of the panel rotated each year, and each store visited quarterly. As a planning estimate, 300 core specs at the national minimum need about 6,000 accepted observations per edition; a 30-store panel visited quarterly with each collector recording five tags per assigned spec produces several times that, so the binding constraint is collector recruitment and review throughput, not statistics. The first edition should cover roughly 300–600 surveyed specs across clothing and household goods and expand toward the ~1,500 the DeductIt user base is used to through parent inheritance, so breadth of lookup and honesty about n grow together rather than trading off.

### What AI agents may and may not do

LLM determinism is unattainable (identical seeds and prompts still vary across runs, and providers update serving stacks behind fixed names), so the pipeline designs for auditability instead ([PromptLayer](https://blog.promptlayer.com/why-llm-evaluation-results-arent-reproducible-and-what-to-do-about-it/); [LLMR vignette](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/LLMR/vignettes/reproducibility-and-cost.html)). Agents **may**: map free-text and photo descriptions to the taxonomy; normalize brand strings and propose brand tier; detect duplicate observations (same store, same tag, same visit); flag out-of-pattern prices for review; extract structured fields from tag photos and partner feeds; draft release notes and triage methodology comments; and propose synonyms. Agents **may not**: generate, estimate, impute or "fill in" any price; decide which observations to drop; edit any published statistic; merge pull requests; adjudicate methodology comments; or write to an edition. Prices enter only from a collector record or a partner-feed record with provenance. The run contract pins a dated model snapshot at temperature 0, keeps prompts and schemas under version control with content hashes, logs every call with request hash, served-version fingerprint and timestamp, caches responses keyed by request hash and ships the cache with the edition so a re-run without model access reproduces outputs exactly, and keeps a frozen audit set of about 500 labelled items re-scored on any model or prompt change with a preregistered agreement threshold and automatic rollback, following the variance-aware annotation protocol in the literature ([arXiv 2601.02370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02370); [Barking Iguana](https://barkingiguana.com/writing/making-an-llm-output-reproducible/)). Each edition ships a datasheet following the Gebru headings (motivation, composition, collection, preprocessing, uses, distribution, maintenance) and PROV-style lineage from every value to its observation ids, pseudonymous collector, store, date and the LLM run ids that touched it ([Datasheets for Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010v7)).

## Tax-year editions frozen by hash give DeductIt a drop-in and auditors a trail

Pub 561 weights comparables by closeness to the valuation date and tells donors using price guides to "use the current edition at the date of contribution," so the value "in effect on the donation date" is the legally relevant one, and existing charity guides, which are undated with no change history, fail that test by construction ([IRS Pub 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561); [Salvation Army guide](https://satruck.org/donation-value-guide)). The edition scheme is therefore `DV-<taxyear>`, covering donations dated January 1 through December 31 of that year, built from observations in the twelve months ending September 30 of the prior year and published by December 1, so each edition exists before the tax year it governs; BLS accepts a 10–12 month lag before finalizing chained indexes, so an annual edition from the prior year's observations sits well within statistical-agency norms ([BLS HOM CPI: Calculation](https://www.bls.gov/opub/hom/cpi/calculation.htm)). A mid-year patch `DV-2027.1` may add specs or regions but never changes an existing value; the published resolution rule is that the value in effect is the edition whose range contains the donation date and, if patches exist, the newest patch published on or before that date, which prevents retroactive changes. Schema and methodology version independently (`schema 1.x`, `METHODOLOGY-2027.1`) and every values row carries `method_version`, so a method change is visible per item and the prior method's value stays in the archive.

Each edition is a Frictionless data package with a SHA-256 over the canonicalized CSVs, a git tag, a GitHub Release that mints a Zenodo version DOI (with a concept DOI covering all versions in `CITATION.cff`), and permanent URLs of the form `/editions/DV-2027/items/{dv_id}`; Zenodo treats each version as "a completely new record with separate metadata, files and persistent identifier," which is exactly the immutability a tax-year table needs, whereas Hugging Face DOIs have been reported to resolve to the current state rather than the minted commit and should be treated as a discovery mirror only ([Zenodo docs](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/deposit/manage-versions/); [HF forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/doi-metadata-and-findability/170350)). The change log is a per-item diff between editions with reason codes (new sample, taxonomy change, method change, correction), generated from git history the way Simon Willison's git-scraping pattern turns commits into a change log ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2020/Oct/9/git-scraping/)). Donor receipts and vendor exports should embed edition id, `dv_id`, tier, value and edition hash, which turns every export into a citation an examiner can verify.

The DeductIt drop-in is a build target of the pipeline, not a hand-edited file: `deductit-compat-DV-2027.json.gz` with `version` = 2027, `itemCount`, the 22 legacy `categories` names (mapped from `irs_group` × `category` by a crosswalk kept in DeductIt's repo), `items[].c` = legacy category, `items[].d` = the legacy description string where a spec corresponds to an existing item (so `findByDescription` keeps resolving old donations) and otherwise the new label in `Subcategory: Item` form so every item is reachable through Browse, `items[].h` = p75 cents and `items[].m` = p50 cents, plus top-level `edition`, `license`, `source_url` and `sha256` keys that the current parser ignores. The four cross-category duplicate descriptions are resolved by qualifying them. Items whose spec is inherited or whose online series is the only evidence are included only if the edition publishes them, which keeps the app from showing numbers the site would not. DeductIt should, in the same release, start storing `dv_id`, `edition` and `tier` on each donation record (the model today keeps only description text, quality and unit value), keep the existing default of "Thrift shop value" as the Form 8283 method, and remove the dead `fmv_data` Firestore rules, the stale seeder and the unused CSV header validator. Adding a third tier later means changing `ItemQuality`, `FmvItem`, the asset keys, the quantity dialogs and the CSV docs together.

The license files are `LICENSE` (Apache-2.0), `LICENSE-DOCS` (CC BY 4.0) and `LICENSE-DATA` (CC0 plus attribution request plus trademark notice), the split is stated in the README and in every data file header, and the governance document commits that the data license will never tighten. If the advisory council or counsel insists on binding attribution, the fallback is CC BY 4.0 on the derived-values table only, with observations and taxonomy staying CC0; CC BY-SA, ODbL and any NonCommercial variant are rejected because the project's purpose is embedding by vendors. Marketing should describe the project as "an open alternative to Intuit's discontinued ItsDeductible," word mark only, never "ItsDeductible 2.0," never in the project name, domain or app-store title, and both the DeductIt and DonationValues sites should keep comparative copy factual.

The contributor instrument is a DCO-style certificate, not a copyright-assignment CLA: with CC0 there is nothing to assign, and an assignment to Foothill AI LLC would make the LLC the owner of the derived values and contradict the neutral-reference story; CERN recommends the DCO and GitLab switched from a CLA to it in 2017 ([developercertificate.org](https://developercertificate.org/); [CERN OSPO](https://ospo.docs.cern.ch/recommendations/CLAs-and-DCOs); [GitLab](https://about.gitlab.com/blog/2017/11/01/gitlab-switches-to-dco-license/)). The **Data Contributor Certificate v1** (draft for counsel) reads, in substance: by submitting, I certify that (1) I personally observed this price at the stated store or listing on the stated date, or I have the right to submit it; (2) I did not copy it from a price guide, app, website export or another person's submission; (3) I obtained it lawfully and did not circumvent access controls, breach terms I agreed to, or ignore a posted store policy or a staff request; (4) the submission contains no personal information about anyone and any photo was taken by me; (5) I dedicate my contribution to the public domain under CC0 1.0 and grant the fallback license in CC0 §3; and (6) I understand the submission is public and permanent and that the project may correct, flag or remove it. The certificate version, a hash of the contributor id and a timestamp are recorded with each submission, as DCO clause (d) contemplates. Bulk contributors (a thrift operator donating a feed, a researcher donating a dataset) sign a short data-contribution agreement with warranties of source and authority, because that is where "lawfully obtained" actually bites. The removal policy follows OSMF practice: a designated DMCA agent, prompt removal on a facially valid notice with contributor notification and counter-notice, personal data removed on request without debate, store or platform objections honored going forward without purging public facts unless counsel advises, contributor withdrawal not honored for facts but de-identification on request, accuracy disputes handled by flag and re-survey, and a public log of removals ([OSM takedown procedure](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk/2013-February/066159.html); [Open Food Facts terms](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use)).

Liability is manageable with the charities' own pattern. Circular 230 reaches individuals who practice before the IRS, not a data publisher; §6701 requires knowledge that a specific document "would result in an understatement" and the charities and Intuit have published guides for decades without reported action; §6700's 200% overstatement prong is a reason to anchor on medians rather than the top of the range ([26 USC 6701](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6701); [IRS OPR 2026-5](https://www.irs.gov/pub/opr-taxpros/2026-5-drawing-the-line-tax-return-preparation-vs-practice.pdf)). Exposure rises at exactly one boundary, a return-preparation flow that auto-populates Form 8283 values with no user judgment, which is a DeductIt product question rather than a dataset question. The disclaimer, adapted from Goodwill and Salvation Army ("prices are only estimated values"; "The Salvation Army does not set a valuation on your donation"), should say that DonationValues publishes estimates of typical resale prices derived from N observed thrift prices using the published methodology, that these are not appraisals and not tax advice, that under IRS rules the donor determines FMV, that items over $500 and similar-item groups over $5,000 carry additional substantiation and appraisal requirements, and that the data is provided as-is without warranty, with the range and sample size shown next to every value ([GII guide PDF](https://wcginc.com/images/pdf_up/Goodwill-DonationValuationGuide.pdf); [Salvation Army guide](https://satruck.org/donation-value-guide)).

## A static, evidence-first site positions the project as reference, not calculator

The search ecosystem around "donation value guide" is a sea of scraped Goodwill and Salvation Army PDFs, and the IRS will not endorse any product: its Tax Forum exhibitor brochure says so in writing, and no private guide has ever been named in Pub 561, which cites only generic "used vehicle pricing guides" published by "commercial firms and trade organizations" that "are not 'official'" ([IRS Tax Forum exhibitor brochure](https://www.irstaxforum.com/document/16/open); [IRS Pub 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561)). The achievable status is the vehicle-guide status: a widely used, non-official reference that practitioners cite in workpapers, that Stakeholder Liaison knows as an industry resource, and that Pub 561 could one day reference generically. The site is therefore positioned as an open dataset with a methodology, not as a calculator, and the per-item page mirrors Pub 561's own credibility elements for an appraisal: description, condition tiers labelled with the IRS phrase, valuation date window, method and version, the specific comparables, and who produced it. The trusted precedents all expose per-record provenance (OpenSanctions promises that "every data point can be traced back to where it came from") and publish bulk dumps with checksums plus a rate-limited API with a "bulk, don't scrape" rule ([OpenSanctions about](https://www.opensanctions.org/docs/about.md); [OFF data page](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/data)).

### Technical stack

Git is the database. A monorepo holds `data/` (taxonomy, observations, values as CSV with Table Schema), `pipeline/` (a Python build that is deterministic given the inputs, pinned dependencies, the LLM cache, the bootstrap seed) and `site/`. CI runs the Frictionless Repository action on every push and pull request and fails the build on schema or logic-check violations, the pattern mySociety and the datasets org use ([Frictionless Repository](https://repository.frictionlessdata.io/docs/getting-started); [publicbodies workflow](https://github.com/datasets/publicbodies/actions/runs/5172776510/workflow)). The build emits one pre-rendered HTML page per item and per edition value (Astro or Eleventy), a Pagefind index (a 10,000-page site searches with under 300 kB of payload), static JSON files under `/api/v1/` served as assets, and the release artifacts ([pagefind.app](https://pagefind.app/)). Hosting is Cloudflare Pages or Workers Static Assets, where static requests are free and unlimited; the free tier's 20,000-file cap means the paid Workers plan (100,000 files) once edition archives multiply pages ([Cloudflare Pages Functions pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/functions/pricing); [Cloudflare Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). The SQLite artifact is built with a 1 KiB page size and query-matched indexes so sql.js-httpvfs can answer a row lookup with about 1 kB of range requests and Datasette Lite can open the same file for ad-hoc exploration ([phiresky](https://phiresky.github.io/blog/2021/hosting-sqlite-databases-on-github-pages/); [datasette-lite](https://github.com/simonw/datasette-lite)). Releases go to GitHub Releases → Zenodo DOI, with Parquet mirrored to Hugging Face and schema.org `Dataset` metadata on edition pages for Google Dataset Search. The contribution path is a small Worker that validates the form, stores the cropped tag photo in R2, and opens a pull request through a bot account so contributors never need GitHub, the pattern Decap's open authoring and form-to-PR bots already use; review is the labelled PR queue, nightly rebuilds recompute aggregates, and automated checks copied from Open Food Facts and Numbeo (price outliers against the spec distribution, duplicate submissions per id or IP, impossible dates, store-type mismatches) are published so reviewers and auditors can see them ([Decap open authoring](https://decapcms.org/docs/open-authoring/); [OFF data quality](https://support.openfoodfacts.org/help/en-gb/21-gestire-i-miei-prodotti/2-come-possiamo-essere-sicuri-che-i-prodotti-presentati-su-open-food-facts-presentino-dati-originali-e-corretti); [Numbeo methodology](https://www.numbeo.com/common/motivation_and_methodology.jsp)).

### Governance and adoption

The trusted commons separate the data from any sponsor's product: OSMF "supports, but does not control" OpenStreetMap, Open Food Facts refuses money from food companies, and OpenSanctions keeps credibility as a company by open-sourcing its pipeline ([OSMF](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/OSMF); [OFF independence](https://blog.openfoodfacts.org/?p=6910); [OpenSanctions about](https://www.opensanctions.org/docs/about.md)). For a small LLC sponsor the workable stack is a separate brand and domain with no DeductIt branding on the data site, a public GOVERNANCE.md, an advisory council of three to five EAs/CPAs plus one statistics reviewer with published conflict-of-interest statements, methodology versions that go through a 30-day public comment window with an adjudication table on the NIST initial-draft → final-draft → final model, a per-release changelog, a Contributor Covenant-style code of conduct, and a rule that no single tax-software vendor funds the project, with vendor support structured as OSM-style tiered corporate membership instead ([NIST SP 800-171 r3 FPD](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/171/r3/fpd); [OSM funding.json](https://www.openstreetmap.org/funding.json)). Legal home: launch from the LLC, because the CC0 data has nothing to own; the Open Collective Foundation closed at the end of 2024, Software Freedom Conservancy and Open Source Collective are software-focused, and Code for Science & Society expects roughly $25,000 a year, so a fiscal sponsor is a step for when external money exists and a 501(c)(3) is premature until a community and funding are real ([Social Change Nest](https://thesocialchangenest.org/the-social-change-nest-response-to-the-dissolution-of-open-collective-foundation); [SFC apply](https://sfconservancy.org/projects/apply/); [CS&S FSP](https://codeforsociety.org/fsp)).

Adoption starts where the gap is. TurboTax's valuation tool is gone and users report no replacement, FreeTaxUSA tells users it has no tool and points them to Goodwill and Salvation Army guides, and the professional suites (Drake, ProSeries, UltraTax) only generate Form 8283 from preparer-typed values with no valuation database at all, so the first integrations are vendors with nothing and the pro ecosystem where a stable ID, a frozen annual table and a signed CSV are what a compliance team needs ([TurboTax community](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/deductions-credits-9/valuation-tool-for-charitable-contributions-is-gone-805578); [FreeTaxUSA community](https://community.freetaxusa.com/discussion/comment/5894); [Drake KB](https://kb.drakesoftware.com/KB/Drake-Tax/12813.htm); [ProSeries help](https://accountants.intuit.com/support/en-us/help-article/federal-taxes/generating-form-8283-proseries/L4O9xzYYt_US_en_US)). TurboTax does not accept third-party injection, so the DIY path is a copy-friendly worksheet and a Form 8283-shaped PDF. The IRS channels are Stakeholder Liaison and its Issue Management Resolution System, the Nationwide Tax Forums (about 13,000 attendees across five cities in 2026) where the specific ask is a dated comment on Pub 561 requesting generic reference to "published guides based on surveyed thrift prices," and, later, IRSAC or TAP membership for an advisory-council member ([IRS Stakeholder Liaison](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/stakeholder-liaison-local-contacts); [IRS Tax Forums 2026](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-wraps-up-successful-nationwide-tax-forums-with-nearly-13000-attendees)). The professional-body entry points are NATP, the AICPA Tax Section and NAEA chapters, with a citable methodology white paper and a CE-ready one-pager on documenting a noncash donation; practitioner guidance already treats the charity guides as acceptable starting points that "need support," which is the exact gap a dataset with published comparables fills ([The Tax Adviser 2016](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2016/jul/valuing-and-substantiating-charitable-contributions.html)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. The hard part of DonationValues is not statistics and not copyright; it is provenance discipline. Every reported case shows judges believing the donations happened and disallowing them anyway, which means a valuation dataset earns its place only by making the donor's record look like evidence: a value with a date, a market, a condition definition and a sample behind it, delivered inside a product that forces the receipt, the contemporaneous list, the basis and the $5,000 appraisal gate. The same discipline is what makes the data lawful to publish, because the only numbers nobody can claim are the ones the project observed itself. That is why the clean room, the contributor certificate, the frozen editions and the AI-agent contract are one design rather than four, and why DeductIt's existing table has to be retired rather than rescued.

The strategic implication is that DonationValues' moat is trust rather than data. Once the values are CC0 anyone can copy them, and that is the point: the thing competitors cannot copy is a public, versioned methodology with adjudicated comments, named practitioners standing behind it, and an evidence trail from every published number to a photographed tag. ItsDeductible held its position for two decades on "thrift and consignment surveys" nobody could inspect; the opportunity created by its shutdown is to be the first guide whose survey anyone can inspect, and to have that fact, rather than any endorsement, be what a CPA writes in the workpaper.
