# Data Rights and Licensing for DonationValues.org (US law)

Research notes, 2026-11-02. Web research by an AI agent; not legal advice. Items marked **[ATTORNEY]** need review by licensed US counsel before the project relies on them. Items marked **[GAP]** could not be verified from a primary source during this pass.

Scope: (a) which secondhand-goods price sources can be lawfully collected and redistributed; (b) copyright / database / contract status of prices and valuation guides; (c) licenses and contributor arrangements for code, methodology, and data; plus liability, trademark, and entity-structure questions.

## KQ1. Copyright in prices and valuation guides: Feist, CDN v. Kapes, CCC v. Maclean Hunter

### Takeaway
Observed transaction prices and posted tag prices are unprotectable facts (Feist); but *estimated* values produced by editorial judgment (Greysheet coin prices, Red Book car values) are copyrightable expression in the 9th and 2d Circuits. Copying the numbers out of the Salvation Army/Goodwill guides or ItsDeductible wholesale is therefore a real infringement risk, while independently collecting observed prices and computing your own estimates is the route those same opinions expressly leave open — and it also means DonationValues' own published estimates are likely protectable (thinly), which is why an explicit open license on them matters.

### Cited Findings
- Feist: "That there can be no valid copyright in facts is universally understood"; originality needs only "some minimal degree of creativity"; a factual compilation is eligible only "if it features an original selection or arrangement of facts"; "the copyright in a factual compilation is thin"; "copyright rewards originality, not effort"; and "a subsequent compiler remains free to use the facts contained in another's publication" — [Feist v. Rural, 499 U.S. 340 (1991)](https://cyber.harvard.edu/property00/alternatives/feist.html)
- CDN v. Kapes (9th Cir. 1999): CDN's wholesale coin prices in the Greysheet are copyrightable; "The prices CDN creates are compilations of data that represent its best estimate of the value of the coins." Kapes admitted using CDN's wholesale lists to build retail prices on his website — [197 F.3d 1256](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.1256.html)
- CDN v. Kapes drew the fact/estimate line explicitly: if CDN "did nothing more than discover and report the prices paid by dealers," those prices "would not be copyrightable." Its prices qualify because CDN reviews publications, selects data, weighs grading and bid/ask information, extrapolates to unreported coin types, and does not apply "a set formula or rule to generate prices" — [197 F.3d 1256](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.1256.html)
- CDN v. Kapes rejected merger: applying merger whenever an idea (a value) can only be expressed as a number "would eviscerate the protection of the copyright law"; CDN cannot own the idea of a price guide but can protect "its idea of what the prices are"; the line "allows CDN's competitors to create their own price guides." The court also said "the distinction between present and future values is not important" (rejecting an attempt to distinguish CCC) — [197 F.3d 1256](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.1256.html)
- CDN v. Kapes: the 9th Circuit noted that applying a formula to data would not be original; "sweat of the brow" is irrelevant after Feist — [197 F.3d 1256](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.1256.html); secondary summary at [Studicata](https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/cdn-inc-v-kapes)
- CCC v. Maclean Hunter (2d Cir. 1994, Leval, J.), 44 F.3d 61: Maclean's "Automobile Red Book – Official Used Car Valuations" was used by CCC to build a computer valuation database; the district court granted CCC summary judgment (no infringement); the Second Circuit reversed, treating the editors' valuations as predictions reflecting professional judgment (year, mileage, region) rather than reported facts, and remanded — [Quimbee brief](https://www.quimbee.com/cases/ccc-information-services-inc-v-maclean-hunter-market-reports-inc); [Studicata brief](https://studicata.com/case-briefs/case/ccc-information-services-inc-v-maclean-hunter-market-reports-inc)
- Merger in CCC (per secondary sources and the CDN court's reading of it): the Second Circuit declined to apply merger to ideas "infused with taste and opinion" (valuations), as opposed to "building-block" ideas; CDN v. Kapes relied on and extended this reasoning to present-value estimates — [197 F.3d 1256](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.1256.html) **[GAP: full CCC opinion text not retrieved — justia/openjurist/casetext returned 403; verify quotes before citing]**
- Creative Commons' own data guidance restates the US baseline: "Facts are not subject to copyright, nor are the ideas underlying copyrighted content"; CC licenses attach to a database's structure and to contents only "(if copyrightable)"; using facts and ideas from licensed contents "will not require attribution" unless the database structure's copyright is implicated — [CC wiki: Data](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Data)
- Intuit described ItsDeductible values as generated from "a blend of data from various thrift and consignment stores," collected "throughout the year" and published as year-end "established values," with condition grouped into High/Medium/Low — [Intuit help page via search](https://itsdeductibleonline.intuit.com/help/valuingexample.html); older (2006) description says the tool searched "an archive of eBay auction data to determine average market value" under an eBay licensing program providing daily closed-auction feeds — [tins.rklau.com (2006 blog)](https://tins.rklau.com/2006/02/i-want-my-money-back/)

### Inferences
- **Copying values from Salvation Army / Goodwill guides or an ItsDeductible export:** the guides list ranges ("low–high") that are editorial estimates, not reported transactions; under CDN/CCC a wholesale copy of those numbers is at real risk of being infringing expression, even though each individual number looks like a "fact." The thin-protection rule from Feist does not help: the thing copied *is* the original contribution (the estimate), not merely selection/arrangement. Risk is highest if (i) the copied values are reproduced verbatim or as a near-verbatim table, (ii) a defendant admits the source (Kapes did), and (iii) the copy is used in a competing product. **[ATTORNEY]**
- Using a third-party guide as *one input among many* (e.g., as a sanity-check or prior) without reproducing its numbers is closer to the "competitors may create their own guides" safe zone in CDN; but the cleanest posture is to not ingest those guides at all into the published dataset's pipeline (see KQ4 clean-room).
- **Observed vs. estimated:** an observed thrift-store tag price, an observed eBay/Poshmark sold price, or a GasBuddy-style field report is a fact. A "typical value for a men's dress shirt, good condition" produced by judgment or even by a non-trivial model is an estimate. DonationValues should separate (1) a raw observations table (facts, CC0-appropriate, no copyright to speak of) from (2) a derived values table (estimates; copyrightable in 2d/9th Cir. under CDN/CCC; needs an explicit open license so reusers are not exposed to the same claim the project is avoiding against Intuit/Goodwill).
- Where the derived values are produced purely mechanically (e.g., median of observations by formula), CDN suggests they may *not* be original ("set formula or rule"). That is fine for openness but means the project's leverage against copycats is the license + trademark + norms, not copyright. Either way, licensing the derived table openly removes the uncertainty for reusers.
- A dataset published without a license leaves reusers (tax-software vendors) uncertain whether the estimates are protected; an explicit CC0 or CC BY grant resolves that regardless of which way a court would rule.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full text of CCC v. Maclean Hunter (44 F.3d 61) to quote the merger/"soft ideas" passages verbatim; relied on case briefs and the CDN court's characterization.
- Found no reported case applying CDN/CCC to a *charity donation value guide* specifically, and no evidence that Goodwill, Salvation Army, or Intuit have ever asserted copyright in their guide values against a third party.
- Circuit split check not done: Feist-era treatment of estimates in other circuits (e.g., 7th Cir. *American Dental Ass'n v. Delta Dental*, 11th Cir. *BellSouth*) not researched; a nationwide project should assume the stricter 2d/9th Cir. rule.

## KQ2. Terms of service, APIs, and scraping: eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, thredUP, shopgoodwill, goodwillfinds; CFAA, contract, trespass, robots.txt

### Takeaway
CFAA exposure for scraping public pages is low after Van Buren and hiQ (9th Cir. 2022), but every major resale platform's ToS forbids automated collection, and breach-of-contract injunctions remain live (Southwest v. Kiwi) — while Meta v. Bright Data and X v. Bright Data show logged-out scraping of public data may escape contract/state-law claims under those particular terms. eBay's API License Agreement affirmatively forbids redistributing eBay Content, deriving category-level averages, and building pricing models from its data, so eBay is "internal calibration at most, and only with a written exception" — not a redistributable source.

### Cited Findings

**eBay (API)**
- eBay API License Agreement §1: "eBay Content" is "all of the information, data, content, images, and other material stored by and retrieved from eBay" and "does not include information that you lawfully obtain independent of eBay" — [eBay API License Agreement](https://www.edp.ebay.com/join/api-license-agreement)
- §3.1(b) intermediate copies "must be deleted when they are no longer required"; §8.1.3 listing data may be at most "six (6) hours older" than the site and other content at most 24 hours older; §16.3 on termination "destroy all copies of, or anything containing, eBay Content" within ten days — [eBay API License Agreement](https://www.edp.ebay.com/join/api-license-agreement)
- §4.2 rights are "not sub-licensable, transferable or assignable" except to display to your own users; §9.7 prohibits selling, renting, trading, distributing, leasing, copying, storing, or modifying eBay Content beyond what is allowed — [eBay API License Agreement](https://www.edp.ebay.com/join/api-license-agreement)
- §8.1.4(1): "express prior written permission" is required to use eBay Content "in any way that enables derivation of" site-wide statistics including "Average selling price or gross merchandise sold for any eBay category"; §8.1.4(2) separately requires permission for "Aggregated seller or buyer data" and conversion rates — [eBay API License Agreement](https://www.edp.ebay.com/join/api-license-agreement)
- §9.5 prohibits using eBay Content, alone or with third-party data, "to suggest or model prices" for eBay listings; §9.3 prohibits competing with eBay Services; §9.10 prohibits using eBay Content "to train algorithms, conduct machine learning" — [eBay API License Agreement](https://www.edp.ebay.com/join/api-license-agreement)
- Restricted APIs (defined as APIs providing "information about market trends, pricing strategies" etc.): §8.5.2(3) prohibits "Sell, share, assign, or otherwise transfer, or license or sublicense" that information to third parties who are not mutual customers; §8.5.2(4) prohibits electronically distributing Restricted API data "in raw or aggregated form" or allowing bulk downloads; §8.5.3 pricing tools built on Restricted API data need eBay's "express prior written consent" — [eBay API License Agreement](https://www.edp.ebay.com/join/api-license-agreement)
- June 2025 update: developers may not use eBay data to train AI or ingest it into gen-AI tools; Restricted APIs cover "market trends, pricing strategies, sales volumes, user behavior" — [EcommerceBytes, 2025-07-18](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2025/07/18/ebay-restricts-developers-from-using-its-data-to-train-ai/)
- Marketplace Insights API (sold-item history) is "(Limited Release)" and "available only to select developers approved by business units"; lookback is 90 days; categories "need to be vetted by business and then whitelisted for that partner"; still v1_beta.2.2 (2020-12-18) — [eBay Marketplace Insights overview](https://developer.ebay.com/api-docs/buy/marketplace-insights/overview.html); [release notes](https://edp.ebay.com/api-docs/buy/marketplace-insights/release-notes.html)
- The eBay User Agreement itself was not fetched this pass; the developer license page at developer.ebay.com/products/license returned 403 to automated fetch **[GAP]**

**Other platforms' ToS**
- Poshmark Terms §4(b) prohibited uses: "copy, scrape, harvest, crawl or use any technology, software or automated systems" and "collect or harvest any information or data from Poshmark's systems"; §7: "Poshmark retains all rights to our intellectual property" and users "will not copy, modify, scrape, distribute, create derivative works, or the like"; §6 users own their Content but grant Poshmark a broad license and "all User Content can be shared with other Users and third parties in Poshmark's sole discretion"; version 4.6 "Effective June 3rd 2025" — [Poshmark Terms](https://poshmark.com/terms)
- Mercari Prohibited Conduct: users may not "use any robot, spambot, spider, crawler, scraper or other automated means or interface not provided by us" to access the Service or extract data; also bars "Selling or otherwise transferring the access granted under these Terms"; last updates listed 1/23/2023, 11/12/2021, 6/29/2020 — [Mercari Prohibited Conduct](https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/topics/account/policies/prohibited-conduct/)
- thredUP terms: both /terms and /legal/terms returned 403 to automated fetch **[GAP]**
- shopgoodwill.com terms: /terms, /terms-of-use, /terms-and-conditions all returned 404; site search surfaced only help pages. A tosdr.org "no spidering" entry that appeared in results is for Adidas, not shopgoodwill — [tosdr point 331339](https://edit.tosdr.org/points/331339). Multiple commercial scrapers exist for shopgoodwill (Apify, Bright Data) but a vendor's existence says nothing about permission — [Apify shopgoodwill scraper](https://apify.com/automation-lab/shopgoodwill-scraper); [Bright Data](https://brightdata.com/products/web-scraper/shopgoodwill) **[GAP]**

**CFAA**
- Van Buren (2021): one "exceeds authorized access" when he "accesses a computer with authorization but then obtains information located in particular areas of the computer" that are "off limits to him"; "one either can or cannot access a computer system, and one either can or cannot access certain areas within the system." The Court warned the government's reading would reach "violations of such restrictions on website providers' computers" and make an employee who "sends a personal e-mail or reads the news using her work computer" a violator — [Van Buren v. United States, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/19-783)
- hiQ v. LinkedIn (9th Cir. Apr. 18, 2022, on remand after Van Buren): reaffirmed the injunction letting hiQ scrape public profiles; "without authorization" does not apply to public websites with no gate, even after a cease-and-desist letter; commentators read it as pushing platforms toward tort/contract theories rather than CFAA — [Jenner & Block](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/publications/client-alert-data-scraping-in-hiq-v-linkedin-the-ninth-circuit-reaffirms-narrow-interpretation-of-cfaa); [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/publications/ninth-circuit-rejects-linkedin-attempt-to-prevent-web-scraping-of-publicly-member-profiles); [Wikipedia summary incl. later settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn)

**Breach of contract / browsewrap**
- Southwest v. Kiwi (N.D. Tex. Sept. 30, 2021): preliminary injunction granted on breach-of-contract likelihood of success based on Southwest's site terms; Kiwi's "hiQ says public data is fair game" argument rejected because contract claims exist independent of CFAA; assent tied partly to ticket purchases on the site; appeal later dismissed after settlement, so no appellate ruling on browsewrap enforceability — [Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2021/10/tos-supports-injunction-against-web-scraping-southwest-airlines-v-kiwi.htm); [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/southwest-airlines-wins-injunction-barring-travel-site-from-scraping); [Law Street Media (permanent injunction)](https://lawstreetmedia.com/?p=15231)
- Meta v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. Jan. 23, 2024, Chen, J.): summary judgment for Bright Data on breach of contract — Meta's terms govern "your use" of its products and Bright Data did not "use" Facebook/Instagram when scraping public pages while logged out; selling that public data also not a breach; ruling is specific to Meta's terms and does not reach logged-in scraping — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-what-does-the-meta-v-bright-data-summary-judgment-ruling-mean-for-web-scraping/)
- X Corp v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. May 9, 2024, Alsup, J.): state-law claims over scraping and selling public data held conflict-preempted by the Copyright Act; contract claims dismissed for failure to allege harm; Nov. 26, 2024 order allowed a narrowed amended complaint limited to *access* claims and again found scraping-based claims preempted — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2024/05/district-court-adopts/district_court_adopts_broad_view_of_copyright_preemption_in_data_scraping_case.pdf); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/10/elon-musks-x-loses-lawsuit-against-bright-data-over-data-scraping.html); [MediaPost on Nov. 2024 order](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/401510/x-corp-can-revive-scraping-battle-with-bright-dat.html)

**Trespass to chattels**
- eBay v. Bidder's Edge (N.D. Cal. 2000) enjoined a crawler making ~80,000–100,000 daily requests on a trespass theory ("eBay's servers are private property, conditional access to which eBay grants the public"); Intel v. Hamidi (Cal. 2003) narrowed the tort to require actual injury to the computer system, after which trespass largely faded as a scraping theory — [EFF analysis of Hamidi](https://www.eff.org/pages/eff-analysis-trespass-chattels-legal-theory-intel-v-hamidi); [EFF ILT: Trespass to Chattels](https://ilt.eff.org/Trespass_to_Chattels.html)

### Inferences
- **Classification of sources for DonationValues:**
  - *(a) Official API with redistribution of aggregates:* **none of the resale platforms** currently fits. eBay's license expressly forbids category-level averages and redistribution without written permission; Marketplace Insights is invite-only and its output is non-redistributable (§8.5.2(4)). The only realistic "(a)" path is a negotiated written data agreement with eBay (or a Goodwill/Savers operator) — worth asking for, but the default answer is no.
  - *(b) Internal calibration only:* eBay API data (public Browse API), if the project is an approved developer, could in principle be used to sanity-check the model without publishing eBay-derived numbers — but §9.5 ("suggest or model prices"), §9.10 (ML training), and the 6–24 hour staleness/deletion rules make even internal retention for a model contractually fraught. **[ATTORNEY]** Logged-out scraping of Poshmark/Mercari sold listings sits in the hiQ/Bright-Data zone for CFAA (low risk) but squarely against their ToS; whether a non-user who never clicks "agree" is bound is unsettled (Meta/Bright Data says no for Meta's terms; Southwest/Kiwi enforced browsewrap-ish terms). Because DonationValues is a public, attributable project with a sponsor LLC, it is a soft target for a cease-and-desist, and after a C&D the contract/tort analysis gets worse even if CFAA does not.
  - *(c) Not at all for the published dataset:* raw scraped listing/sold data from eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, thredUP, shopgoodwill — because redistribution is what every ToS most clearly forbids and because X v. Bright Data signals that the platforms' *own* copyright claims to listing text/photos (user-generated, licensed to the platform) are the live theory. Prices alone are facts, but a bulk dump of listings is not just prices.
- robots.txt: not a legal instrument in itself in the US (no case found making it dispositive), but courts and commentators treat compliance as evidence of good faith and ignoring it (or circumventing blocks) as evidence of "without authorization"/bad faith; the project should honor robots.txt and rate limits for any crawling it does, and document that it does.
- The safest published-data strategy is: (1) first-party crowdsourced observations (KQ3); (2) negotiated feeds from thrift operators; (3) statistical *calibration* against platform data only with written permission or not at all. Any value in the public dataset should be traceable to (1) or (2).
- A middle path to consider with counsel: publish only *derived* estimates (not observations) that were informed by platform data. CDN says a formula output is not original expression; it is also not "eBay Content" verbatim. But eBay's §8.1.4 "enables derivation of average selling price" language is written precisely to reach aggregates, so a contract claim would survive even if copyright would not. Treat as (b)/(c), not (a). **[ATTORNEY]**

### Gaps
- thredUP, shopgoodwill.com, goodwillfinds.com terms not retrievable by automated fetch; need manual read of each site's footer terms and robots.txt.
- eBay *User Agreement* (not the API license) not fetched; it is the document that governs scraping the public site.
- Post-2024 procedural status of Meta v. Bright Data (appeal?) and X v. Bright Data not confirmed.
- No case found on whether a non-registered visitor is bound by Poshmark/Mercari-style terms.

## KQ3. Crowdsourced / original observation: photographing in-store price tags; precedent projects and contributor licensing

### Takeaway
A posted price is a fact and a volunteer's report of it carries no copyright problem; the practical constraints are store photography policies (a private store can ask you to stop or leave, and consistent, posted policies are enforceable as a condition of entry) and privacy hygiene (no people, no staff, no receipts with names). GasBuddy, Open Food Facts, OSM and Wikidata show the two workable contributor models: an explicit grant to the project under a named open license at contribution time (OFF: DbCL + CC BY-SA; OSM: Contributor Terms; Wikidata: CC0), plus a provenance promise ("I observed this / I took this photo").

### Cited Findings
- A private business can forbid activities including photography as a condition of entry and may eject customers who refuse to comply, provided the policy is not a pretext for illegal discrimination — [FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/?p=43937)
- Photography policies are generally enforceable where "clearly communicated, often through signage," and a "clear, well-communicated, and consistently enforced" no-filming policy gives management a basis to regulate recording — [Illinois founders blog (Walmart photography)](https://blog.founders.illinois.edu/?p=13154); private property owners can prevent photography ON their property but not OF it from a public place, and cannot confiscate equipment without a court order — [PetaPixel photographers' rights](https://petapixel.com/assets/store/photographersrights.txt)
- GasBuddy gathers prices "almost exclusively from crowdsourced user submissions," requires users to be geolocated near the station, and in some cases "works directly with the gas stations" to verify; incentivizes with points/raffles — [Harvard D3 case](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/gasbuddy-a-platform-for-crowdsourced-gas-price-data/); GasBuddy's data is licensed to researchers via Chicago Booth's Kilts Center subject to "PDI Technologies' data use policies" (i.e., proprietary, not open) — [Kilts Center](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/research/kilts/research-data/gasbuddy)
- Open Food Facts terms: contributors "place irrevocably their contributions under the Database Contents Licence 1.0" (data) and CC BY-SA (photos); "Contributors agree to contribute only with information, data and photos for which they possess the reproduction rights"; "Photos added by contributors must have been taken by the contributors themselves"; "the public character of contributions cannot be revoked"; contributions "can be removed if they are not conform to the service"; repeat infringers may have all contributions removed — [Open Food Facts terms of use](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use)
- Open Food Facts database is ODbL, contents DbCL, images CC BY-SA; the project chose ODbL so "all derivative or mixed data has to be shared by all contributors and reusers" ("common pot"); mixing OFF data with other databases requires releasing the result as open data — [OFF wiki: ODbL License](https://wiki.openfoodfacts.org/ODBL_License); [OFF API licensing page](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/documentation/docs/Product-Opener/api/tutorials/license-be-on-the-legal-side/)
- OpenStreetMap Contributor Terms are a broad grant to OSMF enabling relicensing, with distribution limited to ODbL 1.0 / CC BY-SA 2.0 or other open licenses chosen by a vote of active contributors; the lack of such a clause is cited as the reason the CC BY-SA→ODbL migration was painful — [OSM community thread on CT/ODbL](https://community.openstreetmap.org/t/on-osmf-contributor-terms-and-odbl-interaction/146187); [OSM wiki: Why You Should Decline (2010)](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Open_Data_License/Why_You_Should_Decline)
- OSMF has a DMCA-style takedown procedure (remove promptly on valid notice, notify the contributor, counter-notice window before disabling repeat infringers); privacy/disputed-data requests go through the Data Working Group; as of 2016 OSMF reported one valid DMCA takedown and zero court orders — [OSM talk: Takedown notices](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk/2013-February/066159.html); [OSMF transparency report 2016](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/osmf-talk/2016-December/004025.html)
- Wikidata: "all structured data in the main, property and lexeme namespaces is made available under the Creative Commons CC0 License" and it "does not accept other data license or designation for imported data" — [Wikidata:Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing); staff rationale: requiring attribution would send credit back to "potentially millions of editors and sources" (attribution stacking) — [Lydia Pintscher on CC-0](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/User:Lydia_Pintscher_%28WMDE%29/CC-0); Dryad gives the same attribution-stacking rationale for CC0 on compiled datasets — [Dryad blog](https://blog.datadryad.org/2023/05/30/good-data-practices-removing-barriers-to-data-reuse-with-cc0-licensing/)
- Numbeo (crowdsourced cost-of-living) is the proprietary counter-example: free for personal/academic/journalistic use with credit, paid subscription for commercial use, no republishing via other APIs or public feeds without consent; contributors grant Numbeo permission to "use, reuse and distribute the data at its discretion" — [Numbeo data license](https://www.numbeo.com/premium/commercial-license); [TOS Tracker copy of Numbeo ToS](https://tostracker.app/document/numbeocom-tos)
- Billion Prices Project scraped retailer sites for research and was later folded into PriceStats and sold as a proprietary product; no public legal analysis of its scraping found — [moneyness.ca](https://www.moneyness.ca/2021/09/a-decentralized-version-of-mits-billion.html); [NBER w22111](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22111)
- DCO 1.1 has the contributor certify the contribution "was created in whole or in part by me" (a), or is based on prior open-licensed work (b), or was provided by someone who certified (a)/(b)/(c) (c), and that the contribution and sign-off record are public and kept indefinitely (d) — [developercertificate.org](https://developercertificate.org/)

### Inferences
- **Legal status of tag-price reports:** a price on a tag or a posted price board is a fact (Feist); the store's only claims would be (i) premises rules (ask to stop/leave; trespass if you refuse) and (ii) in theory copyright in a *photo of a creative sign*, which is irrelevant if the project stores the extracted price, not the image, or stores only cropped tag images. No source found suggesting a thrift store could claim rights in the price itself.
- **Store policy:** Goodwill/Savers/Salvation Army store-level photography policies were not found online **[GAP]**; practical guidance to volunteers: comply with any posted sign or staff request, never photograph people, don't block aisles, and prefer typing the price over taking a photo where photos are discouraged. Consider asking regional Goodwill operators for explicit permission or a direct price-board feed — this converts a grey area into a partnership and is also the best route to "(a)" data in KQ2.
- **Privacy:** tag photos rarely contain personal data; receipts and donor tags can. Store only the fields needed (store, date, category, condition, price); strip EXIF GPS beyond store-level geocoding; don't retain raw photos publicly.
- **Contributor model:** mirror OFF/OSM — at sign-up and on every submission, the contributor (1) licenses the contribution under the dataset license (CC0 dedication for observations is simplest; a fallback license grant for jurisdictions where CC0's waiver fails, which CC0 already contains), (2) certifies DCO-style that they personally observed the price at the stated place/time and did not copy it from a website, app, or price guide, and (3) acknowledges the submission is public and irrevocable (but subject to the project's correction/removal policy). Keep the sign-off record (hash of user id, timestamp) as OFF/OSM do.
- Removal requests: adopt an OSM-style written procedure — copyright (DMCA-style notice/counter-notice), privacy (remove any personal data on request without debate), accuracy (flag and re-survey), and "contributor regret" (not honored for public factual data, but personal identifiers can be de-linked).

### Gaps
- No published photography policy found for Goodwill, Savers/Value Village, or Salvation Army Family Stores; needs a call or in-store check.

## KQ4. Intuit's ItsDeductible export and the clean-room alternative

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- An ItsDeductible export is the *user's* donation records with Intuit's valuations attached. The user's own item descriptions, dates, and charities are theirs; the per-item dollar values are Intuit's estimates (CDN/CCC-type expression). Republishing those values as a table, or using many users' exports to reconstruct Intuit's value table, is (i) likely copyright infringement of the compiled estimates in the 2d/9th Circuits, (ii) likely a breach of whatever Intuit terms the user accepted (standard Intuit terms restrict use to the described purposes), and (iii) reputationally the thing a "replacement for ItsDeductible" can least afford. Consequences realistically: a C&D and takedown demand; statutory damages are available only if Intuit registered the compilation — unknown **[GAP]**; the bigger risk is loss of vendor trust in the dataset's provenance.
- A user *comparing* their own export against DonationValues output (e.g., in DeductIt's import flow) is fine — that is personal use of their own records. Do not upload those values into the open dataset.
- **Clean-room design** (adapted from software clean-room practice; no case found applying it to price guides, but the CDN "competitors may create their own guides" line is the legal basis): (1) the data pipeline ingests only first-party observations and licensed feeds; (2) nobody who writes value-estimation code or curates category definitions has an ItsDeductible/Goodwill/SA guide open — category taxonomy is drafted from IRS Pub 561/Form 8283 categories and plain product taxonomies, not from a third-party guide's item list; (3) the repository records provenance for every observation (contributor id, place, timestamp, method); (4) a one-time written policy prohibits importing third-party guide values; (5) if anyone later *benchmarks* against a guide, the comparison is a separate, documented evaluation step whose output is a report, not data fed back into the model. **[ATTORNEY]** should bless the policy; the goal is an evidence trail that defeats "access + substantial similarity."
- Even the *item list/taxonomy* of a guide can be thin-protected "selection"; building the category list independently matters.

## KQ5. Best licenses for code, methodology/docs, and the dataset

### Takeaway
Recommended combination: **Apache-2.0** for code (explicit patent grant and NOTICE handling that enterprise vendors prefer), **CC BY 4.0** for methodology/docs, and **CC0 1.0** for the dataset (both the observations and the derived values), with a non-binding "please cite DonationValues.org" request and a trademark on the name to carry attribution/trust — because CC0 eliminates the copyright uncertainty around estimates and the attribution-stacking problem, and because ODbL share-alike would deter tax-software vendors from embedding. CDLA-Permissive-2.0 is the runner-up if a license (not a dedication) is wanted; CC BY 4.0 is acceptable if attribution must be binding.

### Cited Findings
- CC 4.0 licenses cover "where applicable, sui generis database rights" along with copyright; CC licenses "are operative only when applied to material in which a copyright exists" and "do not contractually impose restrictions on uses of a work where there is no underlying copyright"; using facts from licensed contents "will not require attribution" unless the database-structure copyright is implicated — [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/); [CC wiki: Data](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Data)
- "CC0 is not a license; it is a public domain dedication"; CC0 "eliminates all uncertainty for potential users" and there are "important reasons to consider using CC0" in science and government data — [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/); [CC wiki: Data](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Data)
- ShareAlike (CC BY-SA 4.0) on a database requires any publicly shared database containing a substantial portion of the licensed contents to carry the same/compatible license, but "does not require you to ShareAlike any copyright or other rights you have in the individual contents" — [CC wiki: Data](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Data)
- ODbL: "You must attribute any public use of the database"; publicly using an adapted database requires you to "also offer that adapted database under the ODbL"; DRM allowed only "as long as you also redistribute a version without such measures"; the summary page "has no legal value" — [ODbL summary](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/summary/)
- Open Food Facts states the share-alike consequence plainly: combining OFF data with other databases means "the resulting database must be released as open data as well"; those who won't share alike "are not allowed to reuse Open Food Facts data" — [OFF wiki: ODbL](https://wiki.openfoodfacts.org/ODBL_License)
- CDLA-Permissive-2.0: share Data "with or without modifications, so long as the Data Recipient makes available the text of this agreement with the shared Data"; "This agreement does not impose any restriction or obligations with respect to the use, modification, or sharing of Results" (Results include ML models); as-is, no warranty; ~500–600 words — [CDLA-Permissive-2.0 text](https://cdla.dev/permissive-2-0/)
- CDLA compatibility FAQ: CC0 data can be included in a CDLA-Permissive-2.0 dataset; CDLA-Permissive-2.0 data can be incorporated into a CC0 work; Results from CDLA data can go into a CC0 work without carrying the CDLA text — [cdla.dev compatibility](https://cdla.dev/faq-resources/compatibility/); ODbL↔CDLA compatibility is unresolved on the OSM legal-talk list — [OSM legal-talk 2022](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/legal-talk/2022-March/009148.html)
- Wikidata chose CC0 to maximize reuse and avoid attribution stacking, while still asking re-users to credit it; a Wikimedia critic calls CC0 "fundamentally irreconcilable with CC-BY-SA" — [Wikidata:Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing); [Lydia Pintscher](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/User:Lydia_Pintscher_%28WMDE%29/CC-0); [User:Amgine/On CC0](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/User:Amgine/On_CC0)
- CASRAI on CC0 vs CC BY for research data: CC BY attribution obligations become unworkable when data is merged from many sources; CC0 recommended for data — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/cc0-vs-cc-by)
- MIT has no explicit patent grant; Apache-2.0 includes a patent license that terminates on patent litigation, plus NOTICE-file, change-notice, and trademark clauses; Apache-2.0 is GPLv2-incompatible but GPLv3-compatible; large companies tend to prefer Apache-2.0 — [FOSSA MIT vs Apache-2.0](https://fossa.com/resources/license-compliance-tools/license-compatibility-checker/mit-vs-apache-2-0/); [FOSSHub](https://www.fosshub.com/resources/licensing/mit-vs-apache/)

### Inferences
- **Why not ODbL:** share-alike on an *adapted database* would require a tax-software vendor that merges DonationValues with its own price data and ships the merged table to release that merged table under ODbL. Vendors (Intuit, H&R Block, TaxAct, smaller apps) will not accept that, and the "Produced Work" carve-out is too subtle to rely on in a sales conversation. OFF deliberately accepts this cost to build a "common pot"; DonationValues' stated goal (adoption by vendors as the reference source) points the other way.
- **Why CC0 over CC BY 4.0 for the data:** (i) for the *observations* table, CC BY attaches to nothing in the US (facts) and only creates confusion; (ii) for the *derived values* table, CC BY would be legally operative (CDN/CCC) and would impose attribution on every vendor UI that shows a value — workable but friction; (iii) CC0 also covers the EU sui generis database right for international reusers. The trade-off is that CC0 gives no legal lever to force "DonationValues" credit. Mitigate with: a visible attribution *request* (Wikidata/Dryad pattern), a registered trademark on "DonationValues" so that *misattribution* or implied endorsement is still actionable, and a versioned, signed release so vendors have a reason to cite a specific snapshot.
- **If binding attribution is a must:** CC BY 4.0 on the derived-values table only (keep observations CC0). CC BY 4.0's attribution is satisfiable by a link in an "about/data sources" screen (reasonable to the medium), which most vendors can live with. Avoid CC BY-SA and ODbL for the reasons above; avoid CC BY-NC entirely (kills vendor embedding, not OSD/OKD-conformant).
- **CDLA-Permissive-2.0:** essentially "ship the license text with the data; outputs/models are unrestricted." It is attractive if the project wants a *license* rather than a dedication (e.g., because counsel is uneasy about CC0's waiver in some jurisdictions), and it is explicitly ML-friendly. Less recognized by non-engineers and tax professionals than CC terms; CC0/CC BY name recognition helps trust with the IRS/pro community. Could be offered as a dual option: "CC0, or at your option CDLA-Permissive-2.0."
- **Code — Apache-2.0 vs MIT:** for a project whose primary consumers are commercial tax-software vendors, Apache-2.0's explicit patent grant and NOTICE discipline remove a procurement question; MIT is fine and simpler if the project never expects contributions from patent-holding entities. Recommend Apache-2.0 (or MIT/Apache-2.0 dual as Rust projects do). Note Apache-2.0's clause 6 does *not* grant trademark rights — consistent with keeping the DonationValues name protected.
- **Docs/methodology — CC BY 4.0:** standard; lets vendors reproduce the methodology in their help text with a credit. If the methodology document is also meant to be reusable verbatim in IRS-facing materials, CC0 would be friendlier, but attribution on the *narrative* is the one place attribution has real trust value.
- Put the three licenses in `LICENSE` (code), `LICENSE-DOCS` (CC BY 4.0), `LICENSE-DATA` (CC0 1.0 + attribution request + trademark notice), and state the split in README and in every data file header (as OFF does with "(c) Open Food Facts contributors").

### Gaps
- No empirical source on which dataset licenses tax-software vendors have actually accepted; the "ODbL deters commercial embedding" claim rests on license mechanics and OSM/OFF commentary rather than vendor statements.
- CC's dedicated Data FAQ page (separate from the general FAQ) was only partially read; the attribution-stacking rationale cited is from Wikidata/Dryad/CASRAI rather than CC itself.

## KQ6. Contributor agreements: DCO vs CLA for a dataset; certifying lawful origin; removal requests

### Takeaway
Use a DCO-style sign-off adapted for data ("Data Contributor Certificate"), not a copyright-assignment CLA: contributors certify they personally observed each price (or hold rights to it) and license it under the dataset terms; the LLC does not need an assignment because the dataset is CC0 and because an assignment-based CLA would undercut the project's neutrality. Pair it with an OSM-style written takedown/correction policy.

### Cited Findings
- DCO 1.1 text: (a) contribution "was created in whole or in part by me" and I have the right to submit it under the project license; (b) based on prior work under an appropriate open license; (c) provided by someone who certified (a)/(b)/(c); (d) the contribution and sign-off record are public and may be redistributed — [developercertificate.org](https://developercertificate.org/)
- CLA vs DCO: a CLA is a signed contract that typically licenses or assigns copyright to a specific entity; the DCO is a per-commit attestation; the trade-off is friction vs. relicensing control; CERN recommends DCO; GitLab switched from CLA to DCO in 2017 — [opensource.com](https://opensource.com/article/18/3/cla-vs-dco-whats-difference); [CERN OSPO](https://ospo.docs.cern.ch/recommendations/CLAs-and-DCOs); [GitLab 2017](https://about.gitlab.com/blog/2017/11/01/gitlab-switches-to-dco-license/); [Kyle Mitchell, "DCO Not CLA"](https://writing.kemitchell.com/2021/07/02/DCO-Not-CLA)
- Open Food Facts' contributor terms require that contributors "possess the reproduction rights" and that photos be "taken by the contributors themselves," make contributions irrevocable under DbCL/CC BY-SA, and reserve removal of non-conforming contributions and all contributions of repeat infringers — [OFF terms](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use)
- OSM's Contributor Terms are a broad grant to OSMF with distribution limited to listed open licenses or others approved by contributor vote; this is what made later relicensing possible — [OSM community thread](https://community.openstreetmap.org/t/on-osmf-contributor-terms-and-odbl-interaction/146187)
- OSMF takedown procedure: remove promptly on valid DMCA notice, notify contributor, counter-notice window, repeat-infringer account disabling; non-copyright disputes handled by the Data Working Group; OSM treats deliberate deletion of freely observable facts as vandalism, not a removal right — [OSM takedown thread](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk/2013-February/066159.html); [OSM help: removing data](https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/65200/removing-openstreetmap-data)
- No source found describing a DCO/CLA tailored to *data provenance* (lawful collection) in open-source practice; general DCO/CLA literature addresses only the right to license — [search summary across opensource.com/CERN/FINOS](https://osr.finos.org/docs/bok/Artifacts/CLAs-And-DCOs)

### Inferences
- **Why DCO-style:** the dataset is CC0, so there is nothing to assign; a CLA that assigns to Foothill AI LLC would make the LLC the copyright owner of the derived values (CDN/CCC) and contradict the "open, neutral reference" story. An OSM-style relicensing grant *to the project* is only needed if the license might change later; with CC0 there is no later tightening possible anyway (which is itself a trust feature).
- **Data Contributor Certificate (draft points, to be reviewed [ATTORNEY]):** by submitting, I certify that (1) I personally observed this price at the stated store/listing on the stated date, or I have the right to submit it; (2) I did not copy it from a price guide, app, website export, or another person's submission; (3) I obtained it lawfully — I did not circumvent access controls, violate a posted store policy I was asked to follow, or breach terms I agreed to; (4) the submission contains no personal information about anyone; (5) I dedicate my contribution to the public domain under CC0 1.0 (and grant the fallback license in CC0 §3); (6) I understand the submission is public and permanent, and that the project may correct, flag, or remove it. Record the certificate version + timestamp with each submission, as DCO (d) contemplates.
- For *bulk* contributions (a thrift operator donating a price feed, a researcher donating a scraped set), use a short signed data-contribution agreement that includes warranties of source and authority — this is where the "lawfully obtained" question actually bites, and a click-through is insufficient.
- **Removal requests policy:** (1) copyright/DMCA — designate an agent, remove on facially valid notice, counter-notice; (2) personal data — remove on request, no questions; (3) store/platform objection ("stop collecting in our stores / from our site") — honor going forward, do not retroactively purge public facts unless counsel advises; (4) contributor withdrawal — not honored for facts (OFF: "public character of contributions cannot be revoked"), but de-identify the contributor on request; (5) accuracy disputes — flag + re-survey, keep history. Publish the policy and a changelog of removals.

### Gaps
- No example of an open-data project's DCO-variant that explicitly certifies "lawfully obtained / not scraped in violation of ToS"; the draft above is original.
- Whether Foothill AI LLC should be the DMCA agent of record vs. a fiscal sponsor depends on KQ9.

## KQ7. Liability and disclaimers: estimates not advice; Circular 230; IRC §6701; disclaimer patterns

### Takeaway
Circular 230 governs individuals who practice before the IRS, not publishers of a reference dataset; §6701's aiding-and-abetting penalty requires *knowledge* that a specific document would understate tax, which a generic, good-faith, methodology-documented value guide does not supply — the same posture Goodwill, Salvation Army, and Intuit have relied on for years. Publish estimates as ranges, document method and sample sizes, and use the charities' disclaimer pattern ("estimates only; you determine FMV; consult a tax professional").

### Cited Findings
- IRC §6701(a) penalizes a person who "aids or assists in, procures, or advises with respect to, the preparation or presentation of any portion of a return" (or other document), "knows (or has reason to believe) that such portion will be used in connection with any material matter," and "knows that such portion (if so used) would result in an understatement of the liability for tax of another person"; penalty $1,000 per document ($10,000 if corporate), one penalty per taxpayer per period; applies "whether or not the understatement is with the knowledge or consent of" the taxpayer — [26 U.S.C. §6701, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6701)
- IRS Chief Counsel advice: §6701 imposes a penalty per false document, not per tax year — [CCA 201805001](https://irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/201805001.pdf); the IRS has assessed §6701 penalties against real-estate appraisers (four penalties against two appraisers in an early enforcement round) — [BVResources](https://www.bvresources.com/articles/bvwire/irs-issues-first-penalties-against-appraisers-56-2); example of a cost-segregation engineer potentially exposed — [Current Federal Tax Developments](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2018/2/5/engineer-is-potentially-subject-to-penalty-for-overly-aggressive-cost-segregation-study)
- Separate §6700 (abusive tax shelters) has a "gross valuation overstatement" prong (stated value > 200% of correct value, directly related to a deduction), with no knowledge requirement but a good-faith/reasonable-basis waiver — [26 U.S.C. §6700, govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/26/6700)
- Circular 230 applies to "every attorney, CPA, EA, or other tax professional who practices before the IRS"; "practice" has a specific regulatory definition and "does not include activities relating to merely preparing or filing returns"; IRS 2026 OPR materials draw the line between return preparation and practice — [IRS OPR 2026-5, "Drawing the Line"](https://www.irs.gov/pub/opr-taxpros/2026-5-drawing-the-line-tax-return-preparation-vs-practice.pdf); [IRS OPR at a glance](https://www.irs.gov/node/7280); no source discusses software publishers or data publishers as practitioners — [IRS OPR 2026-7 in-house](https://www.irs.gov/pub/opr-taxpros/2026-7-in-house-tax-professionals-and-circular-230.pdf)
- Salvation Army (Lehigh Valley) describes its value guide as a tool for "determining the tax-deductible value of any items you are donating" and tells donors to consult a tax consultant about itemizing; the Salvation Army does not put a dollar value on receipts — [Salvation Army Lehigh Valley](https://pa.salvationarmy.org/lehigh-valley/Determining-Value-of-Donation)
- Goodwill-hosted guides state that Goodwill won't put a dollar value on the receipt, that the IRS requires donors to value their own items, that listed prices "are only estimated values," and suggest consulting a tax professional — [Goodwill Greater MD guide PDF](https://www.goodwillgreatermc.org/docs/default-source/donations/goodwill_donated_value_guide-20231013.pdf) (snippet only); [Goodwill guide mirror on jacksonms.gov](https://jfd.jacksonms.gov/uploaded-files/FtFEYi/4OK082/GoodwillDonationValueGuide.pdf)
- Intuit's ItsDeductible guidance: users were told values were estimates ("estimated values" during the year, "established values" at year-end) and that the taxpayer remains responsible for reasonable values; commenters noted values "can definitely run higher than what you might see at your local thrift store" — [Intuit help](https://itsdeductibleonline.intuit.com/help/valuingexample.html); [Claimyr thread](https://claimyr.com/government-services/irs/ItsDeductible-donation-value-estimates-seem-suspiciously-high-are-they-IRS-approved/2025-04-11)

### Inferences
- **§6701 exposure for a dataset publisher:** the statute targets assistance with "any portion of a return… or other document" with knowledge of a resulting understatement. A published reference table, not prepared for any particular taxpayer, with transparent methodology and conservative ranges, does not satisfy the "knows… would result in an understatement" element; Goodwill/SA/Intuit have published for decades without reported §6701 action. Exposure rises if the project (i) publishes values it knows are inflated, (ii) markets "maximize your deduction," or (iii) integrates into a return-preparation flow (DeductIt) in a way that auto-populates Form 8283 values with no user judgment — the last is a product-design question for DeductIt more than for the dataset. **[ATTORNEY]** for the DeductIt integration specifically.
- **§6700:** irrelevant unless someone organizes/sells a plan; the >200% overstatement prong is a reason to make sure derived values are median-anchored, not top-of-range, and to show the range.
- **Circular 230:** no exposure for the dataset publisher as such; the LLC is not practicing before the IRS. If Foothill staff are EAs/CPAs who give individualized advice through DeductIt, that is a separate question.
- **Recommended disclaimer pattern (adapt, then [ATTORNEY]):** "DonationValues publishes *estimates* of typical resale prices for used goods, derived from [N] observed thrift-store and resale-marketplace prices using the published methodology. These are not appraisals and not tax advice. Under IRS rules the donor determines fair market value (see IRS Publication 561); the value of your specific item depends on its condition, brand, age and local market. Items valued over $500 (or groups over $5,000) have additional substantiation/appraisal requirements. Consult a qualified tax professional. Provided as-is, no warranty (CC0)." Show the range and sample size next to every value; version-stamp each release.
- Tort exposure (negligent misrepresentation) to end users is remote for a free, disclaimed reference; it is more plausible for a vendor who embeds the data — which is a reason vendors will want the CC0 "no warranty" language and a clean provenance story (KQ5/6).

### Gaps
- No IRS guidance, ruling, or case found addressing a value-guide publisher under §6701; the inference rests on the statute's knowledge element and the absence of enforcement history.
- The exact current disclaimer text on goodwill.org / salvationarmyusa.org was not retrieved (search returned mirrors and regional pages).

## KQ8. Trademark: nominative use of "Goodwill," "Salvation Army," "eBay," and "ItsDeductible" ("replacement for ItsDeductible")

### Takeaway
Naming a store or platform as the place a price was observed, and describing DonationValues as an alternative to ItsDeductible, are classic nominative fair uses under the Ninth Circuit's New Kids test — provided you use only the word mark, no logos, and nothing implying endorsement; "replacement for ItsDeductible" is defensible, "ItsDeductible-compatible" or logo use is not.

### Cited Findings
- New Kids on the Block v. News America (9th Cir. 1992) three factors: (1) product not readily identifiable without the mark; (2) only so much of the mark as reasonably necessary; (3) nothing suggesting sponsorship or endorsement — [Klemchuk](https://klemchuk.com/ideate/nominative-fair-use); [9th Cir. model jury instructions](https://www.ce9.uscourts.gov/jury-instructions/print/book/export/html/251)
- Toyota v. Tabari (9th Cir. 2010): nominative use satisfying New Kids does not infringe; burden on plaintiff to show confusion; a court may order modifications so all three factors are met but "may not enjoin nominative use of the mark altogether" — [Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2010/07/funky_ninth_cir.htm); [IP Law Blog](https://www.theiplawblog.com/2010/07/articles/trademark-law/nominative-fair-use-of-a-lexus/)
- Circuit variation: Third Circuit has its own test; Second Circuit blends factors into likelihood-of-confusion; Fifth Circuit needs only two factors; SCOTUS declined to resolve the split in 2017 — [Kilpatrick](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2017/1/circuit-split-remains-scotus-passes-on-defining-nominative-fair-use); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2016/11/2016_11_16_second_circuit_expands_split__13324)
- Apache-2.0 §6 explicitly withholds trademark rights, so licensing the code does not license the DonationValues name — [FOSSA](https://fossa.com/resources/license-compliance-tools/license-compatibility-checker/mit-vs-apache-2-0/)

### Inferences
- **In the dataset:** a `source` field reading "Goodwill (Seattle, Dearborn St)" or "eBay sold listing" is pure referential use — there is no other way to say where the price was observed. Use plain text, never logos; add a line in the README: "Store and marketplace names are used only to identify where prices were observed; those companies do not sponsor or endorse DonationValues."
- **Marketing "replacement for ItsDeductible":** comparative reference to a discontinued competitor product is a textbook nominative use (factor 1: you can't identify what you replace without naming it; factor 2: word mark only; factor 3: avoid "official," the ItsDeductible logo/colors, or any Intuit/TurboTax imagery). Prefer phrasings like "an open alternative to Intuit's discontinued ItsDeductible" over "ItsDeductible 2.0" or "the new ItsDeductible." Avoid using "ItsDeductible" in the project name, domain, app-store title, or as a keyword meta-tag spam.
- Because DeductIt (commercial) and DonationValues (open) are both Foothill AI, keep the comparative messaging on the DonationValues site factual and the DeductIt site's use similarly restrained; the risk is a Second Circuit-style blended confusion analysis if Intuit sued in New York.
- Register "DonationValues" as a trademark (word mark; data/software classes) — it is the only legal lever left once the data is CC0.

### Gaps
- Did not check whether "ItsDeductible" remains a live registered mark post-discontinuation (abandonment could matter but should not be relied on).

## KQ9. Entity structure: LLC sponsor vs 501(c)(3)/(c)(6) vs fiscal sponsor (Open Collective, SFC)

### Takeaway
Launching from the LLC is fine for a CC0 dataset (nothing to own, nothing to assign), but perceived neutrality with the IRS and tax-professional bodies improves if governance and the trademark sit with a neutral host; Open Source Collective (via Open Collective) is the low-friction fiscal host, SFC is selective and software-focused and may not accept a primarily-data project, and a standalone 501(c)(3) is premature until there is money and a community to govern.

### Cited Findings
- Open Collective: Open Source Collective hosts ~2,500 open-source projects; platform fee 10% of incoming funds plus payment-processor fees; combines crowdfunding, fiscal sponsorship, and accounting software — [LWN 2021](https://lwn.net/Articles/849442/); [Open Collective docs comparison](https://docs.opencollective.com/help/product/comparison)
- SFC is a "direct project" fiscal sponsor (the project becomes part of Conservancy); applications reviewed by an Evaluation Committee on a rolling monthly basis; projects must advance software freedom, meet 501(c)(3) rules, and be under an FSF-free and OSI-approved license; an older version of the page said "non-software projects to advance the cause of software freedom… are beyond the scope of Conservancy"; projects can leave with a few months' notice; FSA template is public — [SFC apply page](https://sfconservancy.org/members/apply/); [LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/849442/)
- Selenium keeps ~$500k at SFC and uses Open Collective as a secondary host "like a second bank account" — [Selenium update](https://opencollective.com/selenium/updates/secondary-fiscal-hosting-whats-up-with-that)
- Other hosts mentioned: Software in the Public Interest (Debian, PostgreSQL), NumFOCUS (scientific computing), FSF — [opensource.com](https://opensource.com/article/19/1/fiscal-sponsors-open-source)

### Inferences
- **LLC-sponsored (status quo):** simplest; Foothill AI holds the domain, trademark, and GitHub org; dataset is CC0 so no ownership concerns; governance is by policy. Downsides: a commercial vendor of a competing tax app (DeductIt) controls the "neutral" reference — competitors (and skeptical CPAs) may discount it; no tax-deductible donations; the LLC carries DMCA-agent and any liability. Mitigations: publish a governance doc, an independent advisory board (a CPA/EA, an academic, a charity-sector person), and a public commitment that the data stays CC0 and the method stays CC BY.
- **Fiscal sponsor:** Open Source Collective is the realistic near-term option — fast onboarding, transparent ledger (itself a neutrality signal), 10% fee, no governance demands; the data-vs-software question is unlikely to block since the project has open-source tooling. SFC: strong neutrality signal and legal help, but selective, software-freedom-mission-driven, and historically wary of non-software projects — worth an application later, not a dependency. In either case, the LLC can remain the trademark owner initially, with an agreement to transfer the mark to the sponsored project/a future nonprofit.
- **501(c)(3):** justified only when (i) there is a contributor community that wants a say, (ii) grant funding is plausible (charity-sector funders care about donation accuracy), and (iii) the project wants to be cited by IRS/AICPA materials as an independent source. A (c)(6) (trade association of tax-software vendors) is an interesting alternative if several vendors want to co-fund a shared reference — that structure maximizes vendor buy-in but minimizes the "consumer-side" neutrality. Both need counsel and ~6–12 months. **[ATTORNEY]/CPA**
- Note the "max" host and worktree rules in Foothill's global context: organizational structure does not change engineering process; it changes who owns the domain/trademark/DMCA agent record and where money flows.

## Summary recommendation (for the report writer)

1. **Data provenance:** build the published dataset only from (i) first-party crowdsourced in-store/marketplace observations under a Data Contributor Certificate and (ii) written data agreements with thrift operators; treat all marketplace APIs/scraping (eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, thredUP, shopgoodwill) as non-redistributable and, absent written permission, avoid using them even for calibration; never ingest Goodwill/SA guide values or ItsDeductible exports (clean-room policy, documented).
2. **Licensing:** Apache-2.0 (code) · CC BY 4.0 (methodology/docs) · CC0 1.0 (observations and derived values) with an attribution request and a registered "DonationValues" trademark; CDLA-Permissive-2.0 as an optional alternative grant; no ODbL/SA/NC.
3. **Contributors:** DCO-style Data Contributor Certificate (personally observed, not copied, lawfully obtained, no PII, CC0, public/permanent) + bulk-contributor agreement + published removal policy (DMCA / privacy / accuracy / store objection).
4. **Liability:** estimates-not-advice disclaimer modeled on Goodwill/SA, ranges + sample sizes, Pub 561/Form 8283 pointers, versioned releases; §6701 risk is low for the publisher, highest at the DeductIt auto-fill boundary; Circular 230 not applicable to the publisher.
5. **Trademarks:** word-mark-only nominative references; "open alternative to Intuit's discontinued ItsDeductible," never in the project name/domain.
6. **Entity:** launch from the LLC with a public governance doc and advisory board; move to Open Source Collective fiscal hosting early for the transparency signal; revisit 501(c)(3)/(c)(6) when funding and community warrant.
