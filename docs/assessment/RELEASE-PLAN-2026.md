## 3. Definition and quality rules for v2026.0

### Scope and temporal meaning

- Edition **DV-2026**, tag **v2026.0**, method **METHODOLOGY-2026.0**. Preserve the 2027 draft and its history. A correction becomes **DV-2026.1 / v2026.1**, with changed rows and reasons; it never overwrites the first release. Amend `methodology/README.md` in the later method PR so `n` counts approved methodology revisions; drafts carry status/revision/commit identifiers instead of consuming approved version numbers. Method revision and edition correction numbers are separate.
- Target **50** measurable common-item specifications, independently authored. Require at least **25** qualified cells for this proposed initial-values launch. Select from empirical shelf inventory and broad IRS groups, not donor app data or old guide selections. The exact list is future clean-room work, not generated in these assessments.
- Everyday clothing, shoes, kitchenware, linens, books, toys and simple décor are candidates. Each spec needs a unit and objective condition/completeness checklist; exclude luxury/collectibles, vehicles, jewelry, untested electronics and safety-sensitive items. A set and one piece are different units. Avoid brand/premium subdivisions in MVP.
- Publish only 2026 observations. Planned main collection **Nov 30–Dec 31**; compatible, logged 2026 pilot observations may qualify. Freeze final taxonomy/protocol before main collection. Incompatible pilot rows remain outside release calculations.
- Describe every item as a **late-2026 multi-location thrift shelf-price sample**, with actual dates and places/coverage counts. Tax-year label is not an assertion of January prices, nationwide representation, contemporaneous availability or validity for every 2026 donation. January publication is acceptable for the intended tax-year edition. Never relabel 2027 observations or backfill earlier months by inflation.

### Per-cell publication floor — no weak provisional tier

All thresholds below are proposed project rules for public review, not IRS requirements:

| Requirement | Rule |
|---|---|
| Observations | ≥ **30 accepted unique physical-item observations** |
| Stores | ≥ **6** |
| Metros | ≥ **3** |
| Pricing authorities | ≥ **3**, with ≥2 chains/store types represented |
| Concentration | ≤20% of accepted observations from one store; ≤40% from one pricing authority; ≤50% from one metro. Apply corresponding caps to estimator weights |
| Within visit | First five qualifying tags per assigned spec under fixed walk order; do not cherry-pick prices |
| Revisit | No double-counting the same physical item; a ≥14-day revisit is not itself proof of new stock |
| Missing stock or failed criteria | Record absence/failure; publish **no value** for that spec |

Add `pricing_authority_id` and independently documented control relationships: store count alone overstates diversity where one operator's price board controls multiple locations. Do not automatically equate chain name with pricing authority; for example, Goodwill member organizations may differ. Unknown authority/control is flagged and resolved before a row supports a qualifying cell.

Target a panel of **18 stores across six metros and at least three Census divisions**, with at least six pricing authorities overall. This is a recruitment target, not a claim that all specs meet the minima everywhere. It remains a purposive convenience sample. Shortlist spare candidate stores (roughly 24), because some stores or operators will refuse or restrict photography. **Recruitment checkpoint Nov 27:** if confirmed collectors and store permissions do not yet cover at least three metros and six pricing authorities, the qualifying floors cannot be met on this schedule; Neil then decides to delay rather than relax the floors. Fifty specs × 30 = **1,500 accepted rows minimum**; target **2,000–3,000 raw records** with targeted revisits/gap filling. At 40–60 records/hour, this implies roughly 40–75 collection hours plus permissions, travel and review. Planning allowance: about 18 first visits × (3–5 field hours + travel), with follow-up visits paid only inside the approved budget. Track actual yield in the pilot; if it is slower, reduce specs or move the release date.

### Measurement and statistics

1. Record original tag cents, public discount rule and amount, effective unconditional offer cents, observation date, unit, physical condition, working/completeness where relevant, store/authority/metro, collector certificate, permission reference and evidence. Exclude sales tax. Do not assume loyalty coupons, negotiated discounts, working condition or good condition merely from shelf placement.
2. Headline input is the **currently available unconditional offer price**, with generally available markdowns applied and auditable. Retain and publish tag-only sensitivity. State clearly that this is an offer-price proxy; do not call the records completed transactions. Unknown discount mechanics are reviewed, not guessed.
3. Use an **equal-store-weighted empirical distribution**, with the concentration caps checked on both accepted counts and estimator weights. If a cell passes count caps but fails weight caps, withhold it; do not trim legitimate observations or adjust weights after seeing prices to make it qualify. A different weighting design requires a predeclared, reviewed method change. Publish median plus p25/p75 labelled **lower / typical / upper observed sample prices**. They are not condition or brand valuations; no mapping of p75 to “high quality.” Choose and document weighted quantile/interpolation and rounding conventions before collection; retain integer raw cents. Equal store weights give a store with one tag the same weight as a store with five, so the statistics reviewer also fixes, before main collection, the minimum accepted observations for a store to count toward the six-store floor and how thin stores are weighted.
4. Use a fixed-seed cluster bootstrap reviewed by the independent statistics reviewer. Resample pricing authorities then stores while preserving the documented design; a tiny authority count limits inference. Report all counts, within-panel uncertainty, store-median sensitivity and dependence limitations. Do not imply the nominal interval has national coverage or captures convenience-sample bias.
5. No regional estimates, parent inheritance, missing-cell imputation, brand-tier classifier or price-producing LLM. Agents can format, classify under human review and flag; every published price must derive mechanically from accepted facts. Use standard quantile/bootstrapping libraries and a pinned environment.
6. Duplicate review uses item/evidence identity. Two distinct tags with the same price at the same store/date can be legitimate, not automatic duplicates. Keep rejected/flagged history without exposing private data. Review outliers, preserve original facts, and document all exclusions.

Collection starts Nov 30 while public comment runs to Dec 23, so the protocol records more than the headline needs (tag and effective cents, discount rule, condition/completeness, authority) and comments can change the estimator without recollection. If a comment requires a change that invalidates already-collected records, the affected records or cells are excluded or the release is delayed; any renewed comment period moves Jan 15 rather than being shortened.

Future schema/method changes must cover effective versus tag prices, pricing authority, evidence/certification/permissions, safe public projections, sample scope and neutral quantile names. Replace the current misleading `region=US` national implication with an explicitly described pooled sample series. Public photo hashes verify retained-file identity, **not authenticity or public access**: calculation reproducibility is public; underlying photos/permissions may require controlled human audit.
